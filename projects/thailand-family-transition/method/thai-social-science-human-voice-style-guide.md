# Thai Social-Science Human-Voice Style Guide

## Purpose

This guide is for the final Thai manuscript of the premarital conceptual paper.

The goal is **not** to evade AI detection. AI detectors can produce false positives and should not be treated as proof of authorship. The goal is to make the paper read like careful Thai social-science scholarship: clear, specific, evidence-aware, culturally grounded, and understandable to readers outside the immediate field.

## What comparative research warns about

Corpus research comparing AI-generated and human academic writing reports several recurring tendencies:

1. AI academic prose may overuse unusually "academic" vocabulary and ornamental wording while remaining syntactically and semantically homogeneous.
2. AI writing often shows a standardized, low-variance style across paragraphs and sentences.
3. Comparative Thai-language research has found ChatGPT writing more likely to follow fixed discourse patterns, remain generic rather than culturally specific, and use formally correct but less naturally expressive Thai.
4. Lexical markers alone are not reliable evidence of AI authorship. Detection systems can misclassify human academic writing, including writing by non-native English users.

Key sources used to form this editorial rule:
- Goulart L, et al. A corpus-driven comparative analysis of AI in academic discourse: Investigating ChatGPT-generated academic texts in social sciences. *Lingua*. 2024;312:103838. doi:10.1016/j.lingua.2024.103838.
- Zhao N, Lei L. Informality features in AI-generated academic writing: A corpus-based comparison between human and AI. *Journal of English for Academic Purposes*. 2026;79:101629. doi:10.1016/j.jeap.2026.101629.
- Chapimon K, Noyjantuek P. A Discourse Analysis of Thai Creative Writing: A Comparative Case Study of ChatGPT-Generated Texts and Grade 6 Elementary Students' Compositions. *Ganesha Journal*. 2026.
- Angelier V. The pitfalls of AI detection in academic writing: bias, false positives, and the need for inclusive assessment. *AI and Ethics*. 2026.

## Editing rules for this manuscript

### 1. Thai is the default language

Use Thai for the argument. Keep English only when:
- it is the official name of a theory/programme;
- the English term is needed for international traceability;
- there is no concise Thai equivalent.

At first use:

> การเข้าถึงที่ใช้ได้จริง (effective access)

After that, use Thai.

### 2. Prefer verbs over abstract noun chains

Avoid:

> การดำเนินการในการพัฒนาการเชื่อมโยงการเข้าถึงบริการ

Prefer:

> เชื่อมคนเข้ากับบริการที่ใช้ได้จริง

### 3. Do not make every paragraph symmetrical

Avoid repeated templates such as:
- ประการแรก... ประการที่สอง... ประการที่สาม...
- ไม่ใช่...แต่...
- จาก X สู่ Y
- ดังนั้น...
- อย่างไรก็ตาม...

Use them only when they genuinely help the reasoning.

### 4. Let sentence length vary

Human academic prose has rhythm. Mix:
- short sentences that state the point;
- medium sentences that explain it;
- occasional longer sentences where the relation is genuinely complex.

Do not make every sentence the same size.

### 5. Use concrete Thai institutions and situations

Prefer:
> คู่ที่ไปจดทะเบียนสมรส คู่ที่เข้าสู่คลินิกก่อนตั้งครรภ์ หรือคู่ที่เรียนกับองค์กรศาสนา

over:
> ผู้รับบริการในระบบพหุภาคี

Concrete examples reduce generic AI-like prose and improve social-science meaning.

### 6. Do not inflate importance

Avoid frequent:
- “มีความสำคัญอย่างยิ่ง”
- “อย่างมีนัยสำคัญ”
- “สะท้อนให้เห็นอย่างชัดเจน”
- “เป็นประเด็นสำคัญอย่างยิ่ง”

unless the evidence actually establishes importance or statistical significance.

State what the evidence shows.

### 7. Preserve uncertainty in ordinary language

Prefer:
> หลักฐานที่มีอยู่ยังตอบไม่ได้ว่า...

over:
> ยังคงมีช่องว่างทางองค์ความรู้ที่สำคัญซึ่งจำเป็นต้องได้รับการศึกษาเพิ่มเติม

### 8. Culture should sound lived, not taxonomic

Do not present culture as a checklist of variables. Explain how language, kinship, religion, class, migration, gender expectations and generation can matter differently in different lives.

### 9. Keep theory behind the prose

The reader should understand the argument without knowing the name of every parent theory.

Name theories where scholarly lineage matters. Elsewhere, write what the theory helps us see.

### 10. Keep only equations that prevent conceptual collapse

The final paper is social science, not a mathematical methods paper.

Retain the central chain only where it clarifies:
- unequal conversion;
- premarital contact;
- capability;
- service capacity.

Translate the rest into prose.

### 11. Do not treat service use as the only successful outcome

If a person receives adequate information, has a safe choice, and decides that no referral is needed, that can be an appropriate outcome.

### 12. Write for Thai readers outside the field

A public-health nurse, sociologist, family worker, graduate student, community organizer, or interested general reader should be able to follow the argument without decoding English jargon.

## Final language test

For each paragraph ask:

1. What is the one thing this paragraph is saying?
2. Is there a simpler Thai verb?
3. Does the paragraph contain English that a Thai reader does not need?
4. Is the claim stronger than the evidence?
5. Does the paragraph sound interchangeable with any other academic topic?
6. Can one concrete Thai example make it clearer?
7. Is the rhythm too uniform or formulaic?

If the answer to 4 or 5 is yes, rewrite.
