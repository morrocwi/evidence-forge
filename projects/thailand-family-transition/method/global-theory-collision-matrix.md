# Global theory collision matrix

## Purpose

This file asks a deliberately hostile question:

> If the working equations are placed beside the strongest established frameworks in philosophy, social science, public health, family science, implementation science and service-systems research, what remains necessary?

The objective is **not** to announce novelty.

For every framework, the rule is:

\[
AlreadyExplained
\Rightarrow
Import/Cite
\]

\[
RedundantConstruct
\Rightarrow
Collapse
\]

\[
UnresolvedRelation
\Rightarrow
RetainAsQuestion
\]

\[
DistinctPrediction
\Rightarrow
Test
\]

The final paper should be smaller after this collision, not larger.

---

# 1. Capability approach

## Established contribution

The capability approach already distinguishes:

\[
Resources
\neq
Capabilities
\neq
Functionings
\]

and explains why resources/public goods do not automatically become real opportunities. Conversion depends on personal, social and environmental conversion factors.

It also explicitly centres:

- substantive opportunity rather than formal entitlement;
- human diversity;
- agency;
- the difference between means and ends.

## Collision with the current equations

Current working distinctions:

\[
Resources
\neq
Access
\neq
Capability
\neq
RealizedOpportunity
\]

largely sit inside, or adjacent to, this established lineage.

## Decision

### IMPORT

- resources versus substantive opportunity;
- conversion factors;
- capability versus achieved functioning;
- agency.

### DO NOT CLAIM AS NEW

\[
Resources \neq Capability
\]

or the general idea that equal resources can produce unequal real opportunity.

### QUESTION THAT REMAINS

Does service-system access deserve explicit separation from other conversion factors in this family-transition problem because it can be independently manipulated and measured?

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap.**

Capability theory should become a foundational parent framework rather than a competitor.

# 2. Patient-centred access to services

## Established contribution

Penchansky–Thomas and later Levesque et al. already show that access is multidimensional and located at the interface between systems and populations.

Levesque separates service-side dimensions such as:

- approachability;
- acceptability;
- availability/accommodation;
- affordability;
- appropriateness;

and user abilities such as:

- ability to perceive;
- seek;
- reach;
- pay;
- engage.

## Collision

Current constructs such as:

- discoverability;
- navigation;
- cultural legitimacy;
- temporal fit;
- usable access;

substantially overlap this literature.

## Decision

### IMPORT / COLLAPSE WHERE POSSIBLE

Do not create parallel vocabulary when an existing access construct performs the same work.

### POSSIBLE REMAINDER

Two issues may still justify explicit treatment in this project:

1. **private/safe access under partner or family surveillance**;
2. **time feasibility around a major family transition**.

These should be tested as specific access conditions, not announced as a wholly new access theory.

## Collision verdict

**Very major overlap.**

---

# 3. Administrative burden

## Established contribution

Administrative-burden research distinguishes:

\[
LearningCosts
+
ComplianceCosts
+
PsychologicalCosts
\]

and shows that formal eligibility or service availability need not produce take-up.

## Collision

Current concepts:

- entry burden;
- search cost;
- wrong-door cost;
- procedural burden;
- effort burden;

belong substantially here.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use administrative burden for the mechanism explaining why a low-cost service can remain practically inaccessible.

### RETAIN ONLY CONTEXT-SPECIFIC EXTENSIONS

- relationship/family stigma;
- partner-visible access;
- culturally illegible procedures;
- time pressure at marriage/family transitions.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap.**

# 4. Social determinants of health and health equity

## Established contribution

WHO defines social determinants in terms of the conditions in which people are born, grow, live, work and age, together with access to power, money and resources.

This literature already foregrounds:

- housing;
- work;
- income;
- education;
- social protection;
- gender;
- discrimination;
- structural inequity;
- distribution of power and resources.

## Collision

The current barrier vector:

\[
Income,\ Housing,\ Work,\ Care,\ Language,\ Migration,\ Safety,\ldots
\]

would be redundant if presented as a newly discovered list of structural determinants.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use the social-determinants literature for the upstream structural layer.

### KEEP CIVIL-SOCIETY BOUNDARY

The current project should explicitly state:

\[
CivilSocietyConnectivity
\neq
StructuralPolicyReplacement
\]

because many binding constraints lie outside the reach of low-cost civil-society intervention.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap and an important constraint on overclaiming.**

# 5. Life-course perspective

## Established contribution

Life-course scholarship already centres:

- trajectories;
- transitions;
- turning points;
- timing;
- time and place;
- agency;
- linked lives;
- accumulation;
- intergenerational processes.

WHO's 2025 framework additionally calls for:

- person-centredness;
- equity;
- early/temporal action;
- collaboration across sectors;
- continuity across life stages.

A 2026 synthesis of 56 life-course theories/models/frameworks similarly identifies transitions, care trajectories, linked lives, cultural/historical context and non-uniform biographies.

## Collision

Several of the project's strongest ideas already live here:

\[
OneTimeIntervention
\neq
LifeCourseSupport
\]

\[
ClockTime
\neq
LifeTransition
\]

\[
IndividualLife
\neq
IsolatedLife
\]

and the logic of re-contact around transitions.

## Decision

### IMPORT AS CORE TEMPORAL FRAME

Marriage should be treated as one possible transition or access window inside a broader life course.

### DROP STRONG NOVELTY CLAIMS ABOUT RE-ENTRY

Continuity across life stages is already established conceptually.

### RETAIN AS EMPIRICAL QUESTION

Whether **marriage-related contact is a particularly useful low-cost entry point** into later family support.

## Collision verdict

**Very major overlap.**

This is one of the strongest reasons to frame the paper as a problem-specific synthesis rather than a wholly new theory.

# 6. Bioecological model of human development

## Established contribution

Bronfenbrenner's later bioecological model is not merely nested circles. Its Process–Person–Context–Time formulation treats development as the product of reciprocal processes involving:

- person;
- proximal process;
- multiple contextual levels;
- time.

Contemporary reviews warn that many applications underuse the process and time components.

## Collision

The project's dynamic equation:

\[
X_{t+1}
=
F(X_t,u_t,c_t,\tau_t)
\]

and its multilevel family/community/service/environment structure overlap strongly with this model.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use bioecological thinking where explaining child and family development across nested contexts.

### DO NOT DUPLICATE

Do not create a parallel multilevel ecological theory.

### POSSIBLE REMAINDER

The project's service-network/capacity problem is more operational than the bioecological model and can remain as a separate implementation/operations layer.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap for developmental ecology; limited overlap for service capacity optimisation.**

# 7. Family Stress Model and family resilience

## Established contribution

The Family Stress Model already describes pathways such as:

\[
EconomicHardship
\rightarrow
EconomicPressure
\rightarrow
CaregiverDistress/FamilyConflict
\rightarrow
Parenting
\rightarrow
ChildOutcome
\]

Reviews report broad empirical support across diverse families.

Family-resilience frameworks additionally emphasize:

- belief systems;
- organizational patterns;
- communication/problem solving;
- processes that help families adapt to adversity.

## Collision

The current ideas of:

- family load;
- reserve;
- caregiver strain;
- repair;
- child downstream effects;

substantially overlap these literatures.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use Family Stress for empirical pathways from hardship/load to caregiver/family/child outcomes.

Use family-resilience literature for adaptive family processes where needed.

### DOWNGRADE TOLEDO REPAIR EQUATION

\[
Capacity_{t+1}+Reserve_{t+1}
=
Capacity_t+Reserve_t+Support_t-Load_t
\]

should remain an internal formal discipline or heuristic unless directly operationalised.

It should not be presented as an empirically established family law.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap.**

# 8. Nurturing Care Framework

## Established contribution

WHO, UNICEF and the World Bank already specify five interrelated child-development domains:

\[
GoodHealth
+
AdequateNutrition
+
SafetySecurity
+
ResponsiveCaregiving
+
LearningOpportunities
\]

## Collision

The current child-well-being endpoint is essentially aligned with this framework.

## Decision

### IMPORT DIRECTLY

Do not create a new child-quality index unless the research question requires one.

Use Nurturing Care as the child-outcome anchor.

### POSSIBLE ADDITION

The project can examine how upstream family-transition and caregiver-support architecture affects access to these established nurturing-care conditions.

## Collision verdict

**Near-complete overlap at the child-outcome level.**

# 9. Integrated people-centred care

## Established contribution

WHO's integrated people-centred health-services framework already calls for:

- people and communities at the centre;
- continuity across the life course;
- coordination within and across sectors;
- community engagement and empowerment;
- governance/accountability;
- enabling environments;
- services that respect preferences;
- access at the right time and place;
- reduction of fragmentation and duplication.

## Collision

This is one of the strongest collisions with the current proposal.

The following project ideas are not unique in general form:

\[
Fragmentation \rightarrow NeedForCoordination
\]

\[
ServiceNodes + Connections
\]

\[
Continuity
\]

\[
PersonCentredness
\]

\[
CrossSectorReferral
\]

## Decision

### IMPORT AS SERVICE-SYSTEM PARENT FRAME

Do not describe general integration/coordination as a new insight.

### POSSIBLE REMAINDER

The project may still add a problem-specific operational question:

> Can low-cost civil-society and culturally embedded contacts around marriage/family transition act as entry interfaces into an already-existing integrated-support ecosystem?

And a capacity-specific diagnostic:

\[
MissingEdge
\neq
MissingNode
\neq
MissingCapacity
\]

which needs to be located in operations/integration research rather than claimed as an independent grand theory.

## Collision verdict

**Extremely major overlap.**

This framework absorbs much of the earlier “ecosystem” rhetoric.

# 10. Network governance

## Established contribution

Network-governance research already asks how networks of organizations should be structured and governed, including conditions under which different governance forms are effective.

Provan and Kenis distinguish governance modes and explicitly treat network effectiveness as contingent on network conditions rather than assuming that more networking is always better.

## Collision

The project's graph:

\[
G=(V,E)
\]

is mathematically convenient but insufficient as a theory of interorganizational governance.

Edges do not govern themselves.

## Decision

### IMPORT

If the paper discusses a real civil-society ecosystem, it must account for:

- who coordinates;
- who owns referral standards;
- accountability;
- trust;
- data-sharing;
- conflict resolution;
- network administrative capacity.

### CORRECTION

\[
MoreEdges
\not\Rightarrow
BetterGovernance
\]

## Collision verdict

**Important missing parent literature.**

The project was previously under-theorized on governance.

# 11. Implementation science

## Established contribution

CFIR explicitly studies contextual determinants that make implementation succeed or fail.

RE-AIM/PRISM already requires attention to:

- Reach;
- Effectiveness;
- Adoption;
- Implementation;
- Maintenance;
- context;
- costs;
- adaptations;
- unintended effects;
- representativeness/equity.

## Collision

Several current evaluation ideas are already implementation-science standards:

- reach;
- uptake;
- implementation burden;
- maintenance/re-entry;
- adaptation;
- cost;
- subgroup heterogeneity.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use implementation science for implementation outcomes.

### DO NOT RENAME

Do not invent parallel terms for reach/adoption/maintenance unless substantively different.

### REMAINDER

The conceptual paper can use implementation science to test the service architecture rather than present implementation dimensions as part of its central theoretical contribution.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap.**

# 12. Cultural adaptation and cultural sensitivity

## Established contribution

Bernal and colleagues proposed culturally sensitive intervention dimensions including:

- language;
- persons;
- metaphors;
- content;
- concepts;
- goals;
- methods;
- context.

Resnicow distinguishes:

- surface structure;
- deep structure.

A meta-analysis of culturally adapted psychological interventions found overall benefits relative to comparison conditions and a medium effect relative to unadapted versions, while effects and evidence vary by intervention type and population.

## Collision

The project's cultural variables:

- language;
- family norms;
- gender norms;
- childbearing meaning;
- ritual/belief;
- place;
- migration history;
- class;
- generation;

substantially overlap established cultural-adaptation thinking.

## Decision

### IMPORT

Use cultural-adaptation literature for intervention adaptation.

### RETAIN NON-COLLAPSE CONSTRAINTS

The project still needs:

\[
CulturalFit \neq CulturalConformity
\]

\[
CommunityNorm \neq IndividualMeaning
\]

because culturally tailored interventions can still conflict with personal autonomy.

### IMPORTANT LINK

Cultural adaptation should be constrained by:

\[
Safety + Autonomy
\]

not merely by acceptance or uptake.

## Collision verdict

**Major overlap for adaptation; possible useful bridge to autonomy/meaning.**

# 13. Reproductive justice

## Established contribution

Reproductive-justice scholarship explicitly connects reproductive choice to structural and community conditions.

A widely used formulation includes rights to:

- bodily autonomy;
- have children;
- not have children;
- parent children in safe and sustainable communities.

## Collision

This strongly overlaps the project's rejection of:

\[
\max Births
\]

as the sole objective and its emphasis on:

- agency;
- safety;
- culture;
- structural constraints;
- ability to parent safely.

## Decision

### IMPORT AS NORMATIVE CHECK

This literature is especially valuable for preventing the low-fertility problem from sliding into coercive pronatalism.

### IMPORTANT LIMIT

The paper need not adopt all political or movement claims wholesale. It can use the clearly documented normative distinctions relevant to reproductive autonomy and safe parenting.

### RETAIN

\[
FertilityOutcome
\neq
ReproductiveAgency
\]

## Collision verdict

**Important normative overlap that strengthens the ethical boundary of the paper.**

# 14. Phenomenology

## Established contribution

Phenomenology already supplies conceptual resources for:

- lived experience;
- lifeworld;
- intentionality;
- embodiment;
- temporality;
- intersubjectivity;
- meaning.

## Collision

The current distinctions:

\[
ClockTime \neq LivedTime
\]

\[
FormalCondition \neq LivedExperience
\]

\[
SafetyProtocol \neq FeltSafety
\]

are phenomenologically intelligible but should not be presented as empirical causal laws merely because they are philosophically coherent.

## Decision

### RETAIN AS INTERNAL INTERPRETIVE LENS

Use phenomenology to ask what formal structures become in lived experience.

### DO NOT USE AS EMPIRICAL EFFECT EVIDENCE

Phenomenology cannot by itself establish:

- causal intervention effects;
- cost-effectiveness;
- population fertility effects.

### POSSIBLE REMAINDER

It may help explain why cultural fit, formal access or objective time do not fully capture lived possibility.

## Collision verdict

**Useful interpretive lens; not a substitute for empirical mechanism theory.**

# 15. Social capital and community embeddedness

## Established contribution

Community-health-worker research shows that local workers' effectiveness may depend partly on community relationships and social capital, not merely on being cheaper labour.

Family social-capital reviews also describe multidimensional family-level resources related to health.

## Collision

The project previously used broad words such as:

- trust;
- culturally embedded node;
- community legitimacy.

These may partly reflect social-capital mechanisms already studied.

## Decision

### IMPORT / TEST

Do not assume:

\[
CommunityEmbeddedness
\Rightarrow
Trust
\Rightarrow
Uptake
\]

Measure these steps.

### RETAIN

Community embeddedness can be a candidate mechanism, not an intrinsic virtue.

## Collision verdict

**Important overlap and a warning against romanticising community.**

# 16. What the collision removes

After comparison with established frameworks, the following should **not** be presented as original conceptual contributions:

1. resources are not the same as real opportunity;
2. service availability is not the same as access;
3. access is multidimensional;
4. administrative burden suppresses take-up;
5. development occurs across person–context–time interactions;
6. family hardship can operate through caregiver/family processes;
7. child well-being includes nurturing-care conditions;
8. fragmented services benefit from coordination and continuity;
9. implementation depends on context, reach, adoption and maintenance;
10. interventions may need cultural adaptation;
11. reproductive choice must be separated from coercive birth maximisation;
12. lived experience differs from formal system description.

These are established intellectual resources.

The paper becomes stronger by citing and using them rather than renaming them.

# 17. What may remain worth examining after collision

The collision leaves a smaller set of problem-specific questions.

## 17.1 Marriage/family transition as an upstream access window

Established life-course theory recognizes transitions.

The unresolved question is more specific:

\[
MarriageTransitionContact
\stackrel{?}{\longrightarrow}
LaterFamilySupportAccess
\]

especially when marriage-related contact already occurs in civil, health, cultural or community systems.

This is not a claim that marriage is universally the best transition.

It is a testable access-window question.

---

## 17.2 Minimum sufficient support under low temporal feasibility

Established programme-dose evidence warns against assuming that brief support produces deep skill outcomes.

The remaining question is:

\[
LowTemporalFeasibility
\Rightarrow
WhatMinimumFunctionsMustNotBeLost?
\]

Candidate minimum functions:

\[
Orientation
+
Safety
+
Navigation
+
RedFlagRecognition
+
ReturnRoute
\]

This is a design/threshold problem, not a claim that short courses equal full programmes.

---

## 17.3 Couple-visible versus private safe access

General confidentiality/safeguarding is established.

The specific family-transition question is:

\[
SharedCoupleChannel
+
PrivateIndividualChannel
\]

versus couple-only delivery.

The potentially valuable contribution is treating private access as a **structural property of the entry architecture**, not only a clinician behaviour.

This must still be empirically tested.

---

## 17.4 Cultural legibility constrained by autonomy

Cultural adaptation is established.

The specific tension that deserves explicit treatment is:

\[
CulturalLegibility
\]

subject to:

\[
Safety
+
Autonomy
\]

rather than optimising cultural acceptance alone.

This is best framed as a design constraint built from existing cultural-adaptation, capability, reproductive-agency and safeguarding literatures.

---

## 17.5 Return access rather than front-loading

Life-course continuity is established.

The remaining operational question is whether a low-cost design can substitute:

\[
KnownReturnRoute
+
TransitionTriggeredContact
\]

for trying to teach every future issue before marriage.

This is not established and remains a central empirical proposition.

---

## 17.6 Capacity-aware civil-society integration

Integrated care and network governance already address coordination.

The sharper operational problem is:

\[
Bottleneck
\in
\{
MissingEdge,\,
MissingNode,\,
MissingCapacity,\,
Mixed
\}
\]

followed by:

\[
a^*
=
\arg\max_{a\in\{Edge,Node,Hybrid\}}
\frac{\Delta ValuedOutcome(a)}{\Delta Cost(a)}
\]

subject to:

\[
Safety,\ Equity,\ Quality,\ Autonomy,\ Capacity
\]

This is currently one of the least externally validated but potentially most useful pieces of the project.

It must be treated as a decision rule to test, not a proven universal law.

# 21. Current conclusion

The collision does **not** support presenting the project as a wholly new general theory of family systems, access, culture or life-course support.

It supports a more disciplined position:

1. use established theories for the parts they already explain;
2. keep only problem-specific relations that remain unresolved;
3. focus the conceptual paper on the **configuration** of those relations around a concrete transition and service problem;
4. make the remaining relations falsifiable.

The strongest remaining question is:

> Under what structural, temporal, cultural, safety and capacity conditions can an existing family-transition contact become a low-burden route into effective support that improves family and child well-being without coercion, duplication or avoidable cost?

That question is narrow enough to be challenged and broad enough to connect demography, family studies, public health, social science and civil society.
