# Construct system

## C1. Structural Service Availability

**Definition:** The presence of relevant institutional services that could address a family, health, relationship, or safety need.

Examples:
- preconception clinic
- Islamic premarital course
- Christian premarital counselling
- family centre
- mental-health service
- violence-response service

Availability does not imply accessibility.

## C2. Entry Burden

**Definition:** The time, effort, procedural, financial, social, or psychological cost required to begin using support.

Higher entry burden is expected to reduce initial uptake, especially when marriage preparation is urgent.

## C3. Temporal Fit

**Definition:** The degree to which the amount, sequence, and timing of support match the user's actual decision horizon and life stage.

Two analytically distinct states:
- **Rapid / urgent preparation**
- **Planned / non-urgent preparation**

Temporal fit is not equivalent to programme duration.

## C4. Navigation Literacy

**Definition:** A person's practical knowledge of what support exists, which signals warrant help, where to go, and how to initiate contact.

It differs from topic knowledge.

## C5. Safe-Space Accessibility — พื้นที่ปลอดภัย

**Definition:** The practical ability to seek information, disclose concerns, and receive assistance privately and confidentially without automatic exposure to the partner or other unsafe parties.

Components may include:
- privacy
- confidentiality
- individual access
- safe disclosure
- safety assessment
- protected referral

Safe-space accessibility is distinct from a generally supportive classroom atmosphere.

## C6. Cultural Meaning / Institutional Legitimacy

**Definition:** The extent to which an entry point is intelligible, trusted and compatible with the person's lived cultural context and endorsed values.

Cultural context may include language, kinship, family expectations, gender norms, meanings attached to childbearing and caregiving, rituals/beliefs, place, migration history, class and generation.

Religion or faith may be one part of cultural context, but culture is broader than religion. Islamic and Christian premarital systems are examples of culturally embedded institutions, not the only form of cultural legitimacy.

Important distinctions:

~~~text
cultural fit != cultural conformity
respect for culture != preservation of coercive norms
community approval != personal meaning
~~~

Within-group diversity must be expected rather than treated as error.

## C7. Referral Continuity

**Definition:** The extent to which users can move from recognition of a need to an appropriate service without being lost between institutions.

Referral continuity includes:
- clarity of destination
- transfer of responsibility where appropriate
- follow-up
- ability to return
- visibility of next steps

## C8. Persistent Re-entry

**Definition:** The ability to return to the support architecture after the original premarital encounter when a later life event creates a new need.

Possible re-entry events:
- fertility planning
- infertility
- pregnancy
- parenting
- conflict
- mental-health distress
- violence/coercion
- separation

## C9. Modular Relevance

**Definition:** The degree to which content is delivered when it becomes relevant rather than being front-loaded before marriage.

## C10. Functional Family-Support Access

**Definition:** Effective, usable access to support in practice, rather than nominal institutional availability.

Working conceptual decomposition:

~~~text
Functional Access
    = availability
    × discoverability/navigation
    × temporal fit
    × safe-space accessibility
    × referral continuity
    × institutional legitimacy
~~~

This is a **conceptual multiplicative model**, not an empirically estimated equation.

The multiplicative form encodes a theoretical idea: if one critical dimension approaches zero, nominal availability may still fail to become usable support.

## C11. Family Readiness

**Definition:** A multidimensional state of preparedness for entering and navigating family life.

Proposed dimensions:
- relational readiness
- health/reproductive readiness
- practical/financial readiness
- help-seeking/navigation readiness
- safety readiness

Family readiness is **not** defined as willingness to remain married at all costs.

## C12. Service Fragmentation

**Definition:** Separation among relevant services across timing, ownership, entry criteria, information, and referral pathways such that users must reconstruct the support system themselves.

Fragmentation is the main candidate antecedent of low functional access.


## C13. Meaning Coherence

**Definition:** The degree to which a person can pursue family life and use support in a way that remains coherent with their endorsed values, identity, belonging and purposes while retaining safety and autonomy.

Meaning coherence is not the same as compliance with family, religious, ethnic or community expectations.

Working relation:

\[
MeaningCoherence
=
f(
Values,
Identity,
Belonging,

\]

subject to:

\[
Safety \ge threshold
\quad\land\quad

\]

This construct connects the system to Toledo's explicit inclusion of **meaning** within well-being while preventing cultural adaptation from becoming norm enforcement.
