# Working propositions and empirical tests

These are **testable working statements**, not empirical findings and not claims of a newly named theory.

## 1. Availability–access gap

When relevant services exist but discoverability, temporal fit, safe/private access, or referral continuity are weak, usable family-support access may remain low despite formal availability.

**Mechanism to test:** the user bears the integration burden.

## 2. Entry burden

Lower entry burden may increase initial uptake, especially when the marriage timeline is short.

**Boundary:** low burden must not remove essential safety and navigation functions.

## 3. Temporal fit

The usefulness of programme intensity may depend on temporal fit rather than duration alone.

Expected pattern:
- people with more preparation time may benefit from deeper modules and practice;
- people with short timelines may benefit more from a minimum sufficient core plus a clear route back later than from an inaccessible long course.

## 4. พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe private access

Where fear, coercion, violence, sexual coercion, or reproductive coercion is present, private confidential access may support safer disclosure than a couple-only pathway.

**Mechanism to test:** shared visibility changes the perceived cost and risk of disclosure.

## 5. Trusted institutional entry

An entry point already regarded as legitimate by intended users may improve initial engagement compared with an otherwise equivalent but culturally disconnected route, provided professional boundaries and referral routes are clear.

Islamic and Christian premarital systems can be examined here without assuming they should become clinical or violence-response services.

## 6. Preserve core functions while adding interfaces

Where trusted premarital systems already exist, preserving their identity-specific core while adding navigation, safe/private access and referral may be more institutionally workable than replacing them with one uniform external curriculum.

This is context-dependent and must be tested.

## 7. Navigation as an intermediate step

One possible pathway is:

\`\`\`text
premarital contact
→ navigation knowledge
→ recognition of need
→ help-seeking
→ referral initiation/completion
\`\`\`

Each arrow requires separate evidence.

## 8. Re-entry after marriage

A clear route back into support may increase later service use at fertility, pregnancy, parenting, conflict, mental-health or safety transitions compared with an intervention that ends at course completion.

## 9. Safety over relationship persistence

Where relationship safety is compromised, safe disclosure, protection and appropriate professional response are more defensible outcomes than relationship persistence alone.

\`\`\`text
relationship stability != sufficient success criterion
\`\`\`

## 10. Fertility is distal

Any relationship between the proposed service architecture and fertility behaviour is likely to be indirect and context-dependent.

Population fertility should not be treated as the first or sole outcome of marriage preparation.
