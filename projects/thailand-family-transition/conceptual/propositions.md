# Theoretical propositions

These propositions are **testable theoretical statements**, not empirical findings.

## P1 — Availability–access gap

When relevant services exist but discoverability, temporal fit, safe-space accessibility, or referral continuity are weak, **functional family-support access will remain low despite high structural service availability**.

**Mechanism:** the user bears the integration burden.

## P2 — Entry-burden proposition

Lower entry burden will be associated with greater initial uptake of premarital/family-support services, with the effect strongest among couples facing short marriage timelines.

**Boundary:** the core must still contain minimum safety and navigation functions.

## P3 — Temporal-fit proposition

The relationship between programme intensity and useful preparation will be moderated by **temporal fit** rather than determined by duration alone.

Prediction:
- planned couples may benefit from deeper modules and skills practice;
- urgent couples may obtain greater practical value from a short core plus persistent re-entry than from an inaccessible long course.

## P4 — Safe-space proposition

Where fear, coercion, violence, sexual coercion, or reproductive coercion is present, a shared couple-only pathway will reduce the likelihood of safe disclosure compared with an architecture that includes a confidential individual **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe-space pathway**.

**Mechanism:** shared visibility changes the perceived cost and risk of disclosure.

## P5 — Trusted-node proposition

Embedding the universal core within an institution already regarded as legitimate by users will increase initial engagement relative to a functionally equivalent but culturally disconnected entry point, **provided that professional boundaries and referral routes are explicit**.

Existing Islamic and Christian premarital systems can function as entry nodes without being converted into clinical or violence-response services.

## P6 — Core-plus-extension proposition

In contexts where trusted premarital systems already exist, **preserving their identity-specific core while adding navigation, safe-space access, and referral functions** will generate greater institutional compatibility than replacing them with a uniform external curriculum.

This proposition requires empirical testing and may vary by community.

## P7 — Navigation mediation proposition

The effect of premarital exposure on later service use will be mediated partly by **navigation literacy**.

~~~text
premarital contact
    -> navigation literacy
    -> recognition of need
    -> help-seeking
    -> referral initiation/completion
~~~

## P8 — Persistent re-entry proposition

Persistent re-entry will increase the probability that users seek relevant support at later family-life transitions compared with a one-time premarital intervention that ends at course completion.

## P9 — Safety-over-stability proposition

Where relationship safety is compromised, intervention success will be better represented by safe disclosure, appropriate professional response, and protection than by relationship persistence.

Therefore:

~~~text
relationship stability != sufficient success criterion
~~~

## P10 — Distal fertility proposition

Any effect of the proposed architecture on fertility behaviour, if one exists, is expected to be **distal, indirect, and context-dependent**, mediated through family, health, economic, and service-access conditions.

TFR should therefore not be treated as the primary outcome of the architecture.
