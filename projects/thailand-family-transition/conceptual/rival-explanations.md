# Rival explanations and alternative models

A strong conceptual paper must explain why the proposed model adds explanatory value beyond plausible alternatives.

## Rival 1 — Content-deficit model

**Claim:** The problem is simply that premarital courses do not contain enough topics.

**Response:** More content does not solve poor discoverability, urgent timelines, lack of private disclosure, weak referral, or inability to return later.

## Rival 2 — Dose model

**Claim:** Better preparation primarily requires more contact hours.

**Response:** Some relationship-education evidence suggests dose matters, but effects are heterogeneous and long courses may be inaccessible to urgent couples.

The proposed model predicts that **dose × temporal fit** matters more than dose alone.

## Rival 3 — One national mandatory curriculum

**Claim:** Fragmentation can be solved by replacing diverse programmes with one standardized course.

**Response:** Standardization may improve consistency but can also increase entry burden and erase trusted institutional legitimacy.

The alternative is to standardize **minimum functions** rather than all content:
- universal core
- navigation
- safety
- referral
- re-entry

while allowing context-specific religious or institutional content.

## Rival 4 — Health-only model

**Claim:** Premarital preparation should be primarily preconception screening and reproductive health.

**Response:** Health is necessary but does not cover relationship navigation, mental health, coercion, violence, financial expectations, or later parenting support.

## Rival 5 — Relationship-skills-only model

**Claim:** Communication and conflict skills are the main solution.

**Response:** Communication evidence is meaningful but cannot substitute for clinical care, safety pathways, or structural family-support services.

## Rival 6 — Digital-only model

**Claim:** A comprehensive online platform can replace fragmented services.

**Response:** Digital tools can improve access and re-entry, but cannot replace clinical care, safeguarding, violence response, complex counselling, or legal/social intervention.

## Rival 7 — Pronatalist model

**Claim:** Premarital intervention should be judged mainly by whether it increases births.

**Response:** The evidence reviewed does not justify a direct premarital-education → TFR claim.

The proposed architecture targets nearer outcomes:
- readiness
- navigation
- health access
- safe help-seeking
- referral completion
- family-support access

## Rival 8 — Relationship-preservation model

**Claim:** Success means fewer separations or divorces.

**Response:** This is unsafe as a universal criterion because persistence can coexist with coercion or violence.

Safety must outrank preservation when the two conflict.
