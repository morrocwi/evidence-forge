# Prior-work boundary check

The present analysis overlaps with established work on access, continuity, navigation, capability, relationship education, preconception care and safeguarding.

That overlap is expected and should be made explicit.

## Established work that must be credited

### Access as fit

Penchansky and Thomas distinguish dimensions of access and show that the existence of a service is not the same as fit between users and services.

### Patient-centred access

Levesque, Harris and Russell model access across service-side characteristics and people's abilities to perceive, seek, reach, pay and engage.

### Continuity of care

Haggerty and colleagues show why care across multiple organisations can become fragmented and why continuity has multiple dimensions.

### Navigation

Patient and family navigation literature already addresses movement through complex service systems.

### Relationship education

Meta-analyses already examine communication, relationship quality, programme dosage and heterogeneity.

### Preconception care

WHO already frames preconception care as biomedical, behavioural and social rather than laboratory screening alone.

### Safety/confidentiality

WHO and violence-response literature already establish the importance of privacy, confidentiality, safety assessment and referral.

## What remains to be examined in this paper

Rather than claiming any of those ideas as new, the paper will test how they interact when the entry point is the transition into marriage, especially when:

- preparation time is short versus ample;
- a shared couple channel is safe versus unsafe;
- trusted Islamic or Christian systems already exist;
- support must continue after marriage rather than end at course completion;
- multiple family, health and safety systems must be navigated over time.

The reader should infer the value of this configuration from the evidence and equations rather than from a novelty claim.
