# Novelty audit — do not reinvent access/navigation theory

## Why this audit is necessary

The current model overlaps with established theories of healthcare access, continuity, and navigation.

A credible conceptual paper must **adapt and integrate** those theories rather than rename them as if they were new.

## Major prior frameworks

### 1. Penchansky & Thomas — access as fit

Classic access theory distinguishes dimensions such as:

- availability
- accessibility
- accommodation
- affordability
- acceptability

**Implication for this project:** the general claim that "availability is not the same as access" is **not new**.

### 2. Levesque, Harris & Russell — patient-centred access

This framework conceptualizes access through service-side dimensions:

- approachability
- acceptability
- availability/accommodation
- affordability
- appropriateness

and user abilities:

- ability to perceive
- ability to seek
- ability to reach
- ability to pay
- ability to engage

**Implication:** discoverability, cultural acceptability, and timely accommodation already have strong conceptual ancestry.

### 3. Continuity of care

Haggerty and colleagues distinguish continuity across organizational boundaries and emphasize fragmentation as a problem when multiple providers/services are involved.

**Implication:** referral continuity is not a wholly new construct.

### 4. Patient navigation

Navigation theory explicitly addresses fragmented systems and timely movement through complex services.

**Implication:** "navigation" and "connecting fragmented services" are established ideas.

### 5. Family navigation

Family-navigation programmes already connect families to health and social services, sometimes using brief, low-cost, telephone/web-mediated approaches.

**Implication:** this paper must not claim to invent family service navigation.

## What may actually be novel here

The contribution should be narrowed to **the adaptation and integration of established access/navigation theory to the transition into marriage and early family life**, with four additions that are not normally combined in one model.

### Novelty candidate A — Premarital contact as a gateway rather than endpoint

The object of theory is not a course and not generic navigation.

It is the transformation of an existing premarital touchpoint into a gateway for later family-support access.

### Novelty candidate B — Temporal bifurcation

The model explicitly distinguishes:

- **rapid / urgent marriage preparation**
- **planned / non-urgent preparation**

and theorizes **temporal fit** as a moderator of useful preparation.

This is more specific than generic service accommodation.

### Novelty candidate C — Safety bifurcation

The architecture structurally separates:

- shared couple channel
- confidential individual **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe-space channel**

Safe-space access is treated as a structural access condition, not merely a counselling climate.

### Novelty candidate D — Core + extension through trusted faith-based nodes

Existing Islamic and Christian premarital systems are theorized as **institutionally legitimate entry nodes** whose identity-specific core is preserved while cross-system navigation, health, safety, referral, and re-entry functions are added.

This differs from replacing religious provision with one universal curriculum.

### Novelty candidate E — Persistent re-entry across family transitions

Premarital contact creates a reusable access relationship that can be reactivated for:

- fertility
- infertility
- pregnancy
- parenting
- conflict
- mental health
- violence
- separation

The contribution is not generic continuity, but **re-entry anchored to a premarital gateway across changing family-life needs**.

## Revised contribution claim

Avoid:

> We introduce a new theory of access.

Prefer:

> We adapt and integrate theories of access, navigation, continuity, relationship education, preconception care, and safeguarding to propose a family-transition gateway model in which premarital contact becomes a safe, temporally adaptive, culturally legitimate, and re-enterable access point to family support.

## Revised paper design

The paper is best classified as:

**Theory Adaptation + Theory Synthesis + Conceptual Model**

rather than pure new-theory invention.

## Reviewer attack test

A reviewer may say:

> "Everything here already exists in access-to-care and navigation theory."

The response must be:

1. agree that generic access/navigation constructs are established;
2. explicitly cite their lineage;
3. show which mechanisms are imported;
4. identify the marriage-transition-specific integration;
5. derive propositions that established frameworks do not directly specify:
   - rapid vs planned preparation;
   - shared vs private safe-space access;
   - faith-based core + extension;
   - premarital gateway + persistent re-entry.

If those four additions cannot be defended, the theoretical contribution is too weak.
