# Boundary conditions

The proposed model is not expected to operate identically in all settings.

## B1. Immediate danger

When immediate danger is present, the architecture should switch from education/relationship improvement to safety and professional response.

## B2. Referral capacity

Navigation cannot improve completed care when no appropriate service is realistically available.

~~~text
navigation without service capacity
    != effective access
~~~

## B3. Institutional legitimacy

A trusted religious node may improve uptake for some populations but may not be appropriate or trusted for others.

No single faith-based or secular node should be assumed universal.

## B4. Marriage urgency

Rapid and planned pathways are analytically different contexts.

The optimal content depth, sequencing, and timing may differ.

## B5. Digital access and privacy

Persistent digital re-entry depends on:
- access to devices/connectivity
- digital literacy
- safe/private device use
- data protection

Digital availability is not equivalent to safe digital access.

## B6. Language and cultural context

The same navigation architecture may require different wording, examples, referral partners, and institutional hosts.

## B7. Clinical complexity

Infertility, genetic risk, severe mental-health conditions, and other clinical needs exceed the scope of a general premarital education layer and require professional care.

## B8. Legal / institutional environment

Civil-registration procedures, religious governance, and local service capacity can shape feasible entry and referral pathways.

## B9. Coercive relationships

Couple-based modules may be inappropriate when participation itself is coerced.

## B10. Life-course relevance

Not all modules should be delivered before marriage.

The value of modularity depends on the system's ability to re-engage users later.
