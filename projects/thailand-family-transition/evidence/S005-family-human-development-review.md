## Problem architecture

The review intentionally links three domains that are often administered separately:

1. low fertility / demographic change
2. family quality and family burden
3. child and human development

It argues that these domains should be read together rather than as isolated policy silos.

## Distinctions preserved by the review

The review separates:

- desire for children
- socioeconomic readiness
- biological reproductive capacity

It explicitly warns against treating these as the same construct.

## Family-quality synthesis

The review uses recent Mahidol-related evidence to argue that family well-being cannot be inferred from family form alone. Economic security, access to help, work-family balance, care burden, and relationship conditions also matter.

## Child and human-development synthesis

The review treats low birth numbers and child quality as separate questions. Fewer births do not automatically imply better child development or reduced inequality.

## Relevance to project

**Directly supports as a working synthesis**

- the manuscript needs a bridge from demography to family conditions and then to human development
- family quality should be operationalized multidimensionally
- reproductive-health capacity should not be collapsed into fertility intention

**Does not establish**

- a causal pathway from premarital intervention to human-capital outcomes
- that low fertility is primarily caused by any single family-level factor
- that one intervention architecture is superior
