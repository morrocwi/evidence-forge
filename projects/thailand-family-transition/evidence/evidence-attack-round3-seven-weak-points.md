# Evidence Attack Round 3 — seven remaining weak points

## Purpose

This round does **not** search broadly.

It attacks the seven remaining weak points after Round 2 and the three-stream global-theory collision:

1. meaning coherence;
2. experienced access;
3. felt safety;
4. culturally embedded entry;
5. tiered support;
6. opportunity cost;
7. transportability.

Each point is evaluated against the same minimum bundle:

```text
Seminal / established theory
+ current review
+ direct evidence
+ counterevidence
+ falsifier
→ decision
```

Decision labels:

- **KEEP** — retain as a distinct project-level construct or relation.
- **NARROW** — retain only in a restricted role.
- **ABSORB** — merge into an established construct/framework.
- **KILL** — do not retain as a separate construct; keep only as a measurement or implementation consideration if needed.

The goal is compression, not rescue.

# 1. Meaning coherence

## Current project object

The project currently defines:

```text
MeaningCoherence
= compatibility among
values + identity + belonging + voice + family meaning + autonomy
subject to safety and autonomy constraints
```

The key question is whether this is a distinct construct or a bundle of already established constructs.

## Seminal / established theory

### Self-concordance

Sheldon & Elliot (1999) define **self-concordant goals** as goals consistent with a person's developing interests and core values.

Their longitudinal model links self-concordance to:

- sustained effort;
- goal attainment;
- need satisfaction;
- well-being.

DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.76.3.482.

This directly occupies much of the project's "endorsed values / life direction" space.

### Meaning in life

The modern meaning-in-life literature distinguishes:

- **coherence / comprehension** — life makes sense;
- **purpose** — direction and core aims;
- **mattering / significance** — one's life is worth something.

Key syntheses include:

- George & Park (2016), *Meaning in Life as Comprehension, Purpose, and Mattering*.
- King & Hicks (2021), *The Science of Meaning in Life*. DOI: 10.1146/annurev-psych-072420-122921.

This means "coherence" already has a technical psychological meaning narrower than the project's current bundle.

## Current review

A 2025 systematic review/meta-analysis of the self-concordance model synthesized 77 studies and 978 effect sizes and supported the core relation among autonomous/self-concordant motives, effort/attainment, need satisfaction and well-being.

DOI: 10.1037/mot0000366.

The 2021 Annual Review synthesis also treats coherence, purpose and mattering as separable facets rather than one broad "meaning coherence" construct.

## Direct evidence

Sheldon & Elliot's original longitudinal studies provide direct evidence that value-congruent/self-concordant goals are associated with greater sustained effort and later well-being.

This is very close to the project's claim that family action may differ depending on whether it is coherent with endorsed values rather than imposed expectations.

## Counterevidence / collision

The project currently bundles:

- values;
- identity;
- belonging;
- voice;
- family meaning;
- autonomy.

These are not one established psychometric construct.

"Meaning coherence" therefore risks:

1. duplicating **self-concordance**;
2. duplicating **meaning-in-life coherence**;
3. mixing in **belonging** and **autonomy** as separate constructs;
4. becoming a residual "everything subjective" variable.

Meta-analytic evidence also shows that meaning-health relations tend to be stronger for subjective than objective outcomes, reinforcing the risk of circularity if both predictor and outcome are self-reported well-being.

## Falsifier

If a project-specific `MeaningCoherence` measure adds no incremental explanatory value after established measures of:

- self-concordance / autonomous motivation;
- meaning in life;
- cultural fit / acceptability;
- autonomy;
- belonging;

then the construct should be removed.

## Decision

**ABSORB / NARROW**

Do not retain **Meaning Coherence** as a new broad construct.

Retain only a narrower operational concept such as:

```text
endorsed-values compatibility
```

or

```text
meaning compatibility
```

and explicitly inherit from self-concordance / meaning-in-life literature.

For equation compression, this belongs under:

```text
Culture / Meaning
```

as a **conversion condition**, not a new theory object.

# 2. Experienced access

## Current project object

Current non-collapse:

```text
FormalAccess
≠ ExperiencedAccess
≠ UsableAccess
```

## Seminal / established theory

### Penchansky & Thomas

Access has long been modeled as a multidimensional "fit" between users and services.

### Levesque et al. (2013)

Patient-centred access explicitly includes:

- approachability;
- acceptability;
- availability/accommodation;
- affordability;
- appropriateness;

paired with people's abilities to:

- perceive;
- seek;
- reach;
- pay;
- engage.

DOI: 10.1186/1475-9276-12-18.

### Fortney et al.

The 21st-century access model explicitly distinguishes:

- **actual access**;
- **perceived access**;
- utilization;
- quality.

Temporal, geographical, financial and digital access can each have actual and perceived components.

## Current review

A 2025 scoping review of behavioral-health accessibility measures concludes that perceived accessibility is already a recognized access dimension and recommends using established access frameworks such as Levesque or Fortney.

The review also identifies the Assessment of Perceived Access to Care (APAC) as one of the more generic multidimensional perceived-access instruments.

## Direct evidence

Fortney et al.'s APAC validation study (n=1004 community-health-center patients screening positive for psychiatric disorders) found:

- acceptable psychometric properties;
- expected associations with social determinants of access;
- better perceived access correlated with more outpatient mental-health visits.

PMID: 33165120.

This establishes that "experienced/perceived access" is measurable and can relate to utilization.

## Counterevidence / collision

The global access literature already contains perceived access.

A new construct called **Experienced Access** risks being only a relabeling of:

- perceived accessibility;
- acceptability;
- approachability;
- accommodation;
- trust;
- burden.

If the project uses "experienced access" as a phenomenological reminder, that is useful analytically, but it does not require a new variable in the final theory.

## Falsifier

If a new experienced-access measure provides no incremental prediction of:

- initiation;
- completion;
- disclosure;
- re-entry;

beyond established perceived-access measures, it should be removed.

## Decision

**ABSORB**

Merge into **Effective Access / Perceived Access**.

Retain:

```text
formal availability
≠ perceived access
≠ realized utilization
```

but do not claim a new experienced-access construct.

# 3. Felt safety

## Current project object

Current non-collapse:

```text
SafetyProtocol
≠ FeltSafety
```

The intended meaning is whether a person experiences the setting as safe enough to disclose or act.

## Seminal / established neighbours

This object overlaps several established families:

- perceived safety;
- trust;
- confidentiality;
- privacy;
- trauma-informed / survivor-centred care;
- psychological safety.

However, **organizational psychological safety** is mainly a team/workplace construct and should not be imported wholesale into survivor disclosure.

The closest evidence for this project comes from intimate-partner/domestic-violence disclosure literature.

## Current review

Heron & Eisma's systematic review of 34 qualitative studies (783 victims) found that disclosure barriers included:

- perceived lack of safety;
- fear of consequences;
- negative provider attitudes;
- lack of privacy;
- lack of trust.

Facilitators included:

- positive/trusting provider relationship;
- direct inquiry;
- safe environment;
- confidentiality.

DOI: 10.1111/hsc.13282.

A newer 2026 scoping review extends the disclosure-barrier literature to broader victim and provider perspectives and more culturally diverse settings.

## Direct evidence

Across the primary qualitative studies synthesized by Heron & Eisma, victims reported being more likely to disclose where they:

- felt the setting was safe;
- trusted the professional;
- expected confidentiality;
- had privacy.

This supports a subjective safety readout.

## Counterevidence / collision

The evidence does **not** yet show that "felt safety" is a distinct general construct independent of:

- trust;
- confidentiality;
- privacy;
- perceived consequences;
- provider competence;
- coercive surveillance.

A service may also need objective safety procedures even when a user reports feeling safe.

Therefore:

```text
felt safe
≠ objectively protected
```

in both directions.

## Falsifier

If perceived/felt safety adds no incremental explanatory value for disclosure/help-seeking after trust, privacy, confidentiality and fear of consequences are measured, it should collapse.

## Decision

**NARROW / ABSORB**

Do not retain a broad generic **Felt Safety** construct.

Retain **perceived safety for disclosure/use** as a lived readout inside:

```text
Safety + Private Access
```

This remains especially relevant under coercion, surveillance and stigma.

# 4. Culturally embedded entry

## Current project object

Candidate proposition:

```text
Trusted / culturally embedded entry point
→ lower entry burden or higher engagement
```

Examples include mosques, churches, temples, ethnic/migrant organizations, women's groups, community associations and other trusted institutions.

## Seminal / established theory

### Cultural adaptation frameworks

The cultural-adaptation literature already distinguishes intervention adaptation across:

- language;
- persons;
- metaphors;
- content;
- concepts;
- goals;
- methods;
- context.

The Bernal ecological-validity tradition and later cultural-adaptation frameworks already cover much of the "fit" mechanism.

### Community health workers / trusted messengers

Community-health-worker literature explicitly uses workers who are trusted members of the communities they serve as bridges into care.

## Current reviews

### Faith-community engagement

A 2025 systematic review of public-health engagement with faith communities screened 13,117 articles and included 121 articles representing 96 projects.

It shows that public-health programs frequently work through faith communities, but the mechanisms and outcomes differ substantially by topic and design.

### Mosque-based interventions

A 2024 scoping review identified 14 mosque-based public-health intervention studies. It reports potential for culturally sensitive delivery and communication while also noting that individual mosques are not automatically suitable settings and that stronger theory/evaluation are needed.

### Culturally adapted digital interventions

A 2026 systematic review of culturally adapted digital mental-health interventions found better engagement patterns where adaptations were deeper and participatory rather than surface translation alone.

## Direct evidence

Evidence families indicate that culturally/community embedded delivery can:

- tailor communication;
- mitigate barriers;
- build trust;
- increase reach/engagement;
- sometimes improve uptake.

Faith-based organization reviews and community-health-worker studies provide direct examples.

## Counterevidence

A 2022 meta-analysis of culturally adapted digital health-promotion interventions found no overall superiority over active controls for short- or long-term behavior outcomes, despite some subgroup benefits.

Therefore:

```text
Cultural adaptation
≠ automatic effectiveness
```

and:

```text
Culturally embedded institution
≠ trusted/safe for every member
```

A mosque/church/community body may also carry:

- confidentiality concerns;
- hierarchy;
- stigma;
- gender/power asymmetries;
- low professional capacity.

## Falsifier

If culturally embedded entry points do not improve:

- reach;
- initial engagement;
- trust;
- referral completion;

relative to otherwise comparable neutral channels—or if they reduce private/safe access—the proposition should be rejected in that context.

## Decision

**KEEP / NARROW AS IMPLEMENTATION CONDITION**

Do not create a general "culturally embedded entry" theory.

Retain it as a contingent implementation strategy under:

```text
CulturalMeaning
+
Trust
+
Safety
+
ProfessionalBoundary
```

The node must earn trust empirically; cultural embeddedness alone is not enough.

# 5. Tiered support

## Current project object

Current service architecture proposes:

```text
Tier 0 information infrastructure
Tier 1 community support
Tier 2 targeted professional support
Tier 3 specialist/protective response
```

and asks whether:

```text
UniversalLightTouch + TargetedIntensity
?<
UniversalHighIntensity
```

in cost for meaningful outcomes.

## Seminal / established theory

### Stepped care

Bower & Gilbody (2005) formalized stepped care around:

- least restrictive / least intensive effective intervention;
- efficiency;
- monitoring;
- stepping up when needed.

DOI: 10.1192/bjp.186.1.11.

### Proportionate universalism

The Marmot tradition proposes universal action with intensity proportional to disadvantage.

This is already a direct intellectual ancestor of "universal light touch + targeted intensity."

## Current reviews

A 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of 41 stepped-care studies (31 in meta-analysis) found benefits over usual care for several mental-health outcomes, including depression response/remission, but also substantial heterogeneity and mixed results for some outcomes.

DOI: 10.1186/s13033-026-00717-w.

A review of proportionate universalism found substantial conceptual resonance but relatively few clear implementations and important unresolved issues around:

- defining proportionality;
- identifying need;
- evaluation;
- equity.

## Direct evidence

Stepped-care trials provide direct evidence that sequential intensity can work in defined mental-health pathways.

There are also family-resilience/parenting examples in proportionate-intervention trials, but the evidence is much thinner than in mental health.

## Counterevidence

Tiering can fail through:

- inaccurate triage;
- false negatives;
- under-treatment;
- administrative burden;
- people being lost between steps;
- hidden coordination/supervision costs;
- inequity in escalation.

The family-support system is also broader than a single clinical pathway.

## Falsifier

If the tiered architecture:

- misses high-risk families;
- delays appropriate specialist response;
- does not lower total cost;
- increases inequality;
- produces no better outcomes than universal provision;

then tiering should not be retained for that function.

## Decision

**ABSORB / RETAIN AS IMPLEMENTATION ARCHITECTURE**

The theory is already:

```text
stepped care
+
proportionate universalism
```

The project's contribution is only the specific family-transition application and escalation rules.

Do not present tiered support as a new construct.

# 6. Opportunity cost

## Current project object

The project worries that accessing support can consume:

- time;
- travel;
- work;
- caregiving;
- energy;
- income;
- attention.

This is sometimes described as opportunity cost.

## Seminal / established theory

Opportunity cost is a foundational economic concept and needs no new theoretical construct here.

Health-services literature has long treated patient time as a real resource cost.

Fortney's access model explicitly includes temporal burden and opportunity costs as dimensions of access.

## Current review

A 2025 systematic review of burden-of-treatment theory confirms that healthcare workload includes the actions/resources patients devote to care and the impact of that workload on capacity and well-being.

A 2025 systematic review of digital interventions and treatment burden also finds that interventions can reduce some burdens while simultaneously creating new patient workload.

## Direct evidence

A nationally representative US analysis of ambulatory care estimated an average total visit time of about two hours and substantial patient opportunity cost; the authors concluded that patient time can exceed direct out-of-pocket payment and is relevant to healthcare efficiency.

Other health-economic work shows private opportunity cost of time can predict healthcare demand.

## Counterevidence

Reducing time/opportunity costs can increase:

- appropriate access;
- but also potentially low-value or unnecessary use.

Opportunity cost also varies by:

- employment;
- caregiving;
- income;
- travel;
- flexibility;
- subjective valuation.

Therefore a single money conversion can obscure distributional differences.

## Falsifier

There is no useful falsifier for "opportunity cost exists"—it is an accounting/economic principle.

The only project-specific test is whether adding a separate `OpportunityCost` variable improves prediction or decision-making beyond:

- temporal access;
- entry burden;
- treatment burden;
- financial cost;
- workload/capacity.

## Decision

**KILL AS SEPARATE CONSTRUCT**

Absorb into:

```text
EntryBurden
+
TemporalAccess
+
Financial/WorkloadCost
```

and into the intervention cost function.

Do not allocate a separate core theory node to opportunity cost.

# 7. Transportability

## Current project object

The project needs to know whether evidence from:

- another country;
- another religious/cultural context;
- another service system;
- another life transition;
- another population;

can be used in the Thailand family-transition argument.

## Seminal / established theory

### RE-AIM

RE-AIM was introduced in 1999 specifically to address public-health impact and external validity across:

- reach;
- effectiveness;
- adoption;
- implementation;
- maintenance.

### CFIR

CFIR (2009) integrates determinants across:

- intervention;
- inner setting;
- outer setting;
- individuals;
- implementation process.

It explicitly addresses why interventions may work differently across contexts.

DOI: 10.1186/1748-5908-4-50.

## Current reviews

A 2023 Annual Review synthesizes modern causal methods for generalizability and transportability and emphasizes that transportability is always relative to a defined **target population**.

A 2025 systematic review of applied transportability/generalizability analyses identified 68 publications, 83 source-target dataset pairs and 99 analyses, while noting substantial variation in application and reporting.

## Direct evidence

The applied literature demonstrates that effect estimates can be reweighted/transported when:

- source and target populations are explicitly defined;
- effect modifiers are measured;
- assumptions are stated;
- sufficient source-target overlap exists.

## Counterevidence

Transportability is not a property a study simply "has."

A study may transport to one target but not another.

Implementation-science literature also shows that context can modify:

- reach;
- adoption;
- fidelity;
- adaptation;
- sustainability;
- effects.

Therefore "this worked elsewhere" is not enough.

## Falsifier

A transport claim fails when:

- target context is not specified;
- key effect modifiers differ and are unmeasured;
- there is poor source-target overlap;
- mechanisms depend on institutions absent in the target;
- replicated local evidence reverses the expected direction.

## Decision

**KILL AS PROJECT CONSTRUCT / RETAIN AS EVIDENCE GATE**

Transportability belongs to established:

```text
external validity
+
implementation science
+
causal transportability
```

It should become a mandatory field on evidence cards, not a separate theory variable.

Every foreign source should record:

```text
Source population/context
Target population/context
Mechanism assumed portable
Known differences
Transport risk
```

# 9. Compression consequence

After Round 3, five of the seven weak points should **not** survive as independent theoretical constructs.

A more disciplined core remains:

```text
Structure
→ EffectivePossibility
→ Capability
→ Action
→ Family / Child Outcomes
```

with conversion conditions approximately grouped as:

```text
EffectivePossibility
= f(
  Access,
  Time/Burden,
  Safety/Autonomy,
  Culture/Meaning,
  Capacity/Continuity
)
```

Interpretation after collision:

- **Access** already includes perceived/experienced access.
- **Time/Burden** absorbs opportunity cost and temporal feasibility.
- **Safety/Autonomy** includes objective safeguards + perceived disclosure safety.
- **Culture/Meaning** includes values compatibility, not a new "meaning coherence" theory.
- **Capacity/Continuity** includes re-entry, service load and edge/node/capacity diagnosis.
- **Tiering** is an implementation architecture, not a state variable.
- **Transportability** is an evidence gate, not a causal variable.

This is a **compression recommendation**, not a newly registered Toledo equation.

# 10. What remains genuinely project-specific after Round 3

The strongest unresolved project-specific propositions are now narrower:

1. **family-transition contact as an access window**;
2. **minimum sufficient functions when time/burden is constrained**;
3. **shared + private channels versus shared-only pathways under coercion/privacy risk**;
4. **known return route + triggered contact as a low-burden continuity design**;
5. **capacity-aware referral/interface design at culturally meaningful entry points**.

These are much more defensible than presenting every internal analytic distinction as a new construct.
