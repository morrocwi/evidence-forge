# S001 evidence note — Thailand population situation 2026

## Source identity

**Title:** สถานการณ์ประชากรไทย ปี 2569: “พลิกวิกฤตเกิดน้อย–สังคมสูงวัย สู่การลงทุนใน ‘คน’ เพื่ออนาคตประเทศ”  
**Institution:** Institute for Population and Social Research, Mahidol University  
**Source type:** institutional demographic report / press briefing  
**Status:** primary file reviewed

## Core observations

The report frames Thailand's population transition as a combination of:

- low births
- population ageing
- contraction of the future labour force

For 2025, the report states:

- 416,574 births
- 559,684 deaths
- negative natural increase for the fifth consecutive year

It also presents a conditional projection: if Thailand's total fertility rate continues falling toward 0.7 within the next decade, the population could fall to about 61.9 million by 2035, with people aged 60+ exceeding 28% and the working-age population shrinking by about 3.4 million.

## Family-support framing

The report's public-opinion section indicates that economic insecurity, cost of living, debt, insufficient accessible childcare, and inflexible working time are important constraints around childbearing. It explicitly argues that cash transfers alone are insufficient and highlights education quality, flexible work, and family-support systems.

## Strategic framing

The report does not frame the response only as increasing births. It calls for long-term investment in human quality, including early childhood, workforce skills, and a family-supportive ecosystem.

## Relevance to project

**Directly supports**

- low fertility should be discussed together with ageing and labour-force change
- family-support conditions are part of the contemporary Thai low-fertility problem framing
- a human-development response is compatible with the report's stated direction

**Does not directly support**

- that premarital education increases fertility
- that a specific premarital intervention will change national birth rates
- that any single family-support policy is sufficient
