# Global citation attack — Round 1

## Purpose

This file records the first cross-domain challenge to the equation-first reconstruction.

It is not a bibliography dump. Each source is recorded against the claim it can and cannot support.

---

## A. International policy portfolios and the failure of simple input → fertility logic

### Singapore

**Official sources**

1. Singapore Government, Population.gov.sg. *Marriage & Parenthood Measures at National Day Rally 2026* (23 Aug 2026).
2. Ministry of Manpower. *Shared parental leave*.
3. Ministry of Health. *Marriage and Parenthood Schemes*.
4. Ministry of Social and Family Development. *Families for Life empowers couples with enhanced marriage support initiatives* (2025).
5. Registry of Muslim Marriages / Our Marriage Journey. *Face-to-Face session (Bersamamu) with Kadi/Naib Kadi*.

**Direct support**

- Singapore combines financial, leave, childcare/preschool, housing, fertility-treatment and marriage-support instruments.
- Eligible births from 1 Apr 2026 receive 10 weeks of shared parental leave.
- Public assisted conception is co-funded for eligible couples.
- Marriage programmes include communication, conflict resolution, finances, family planning/fertility awareness and related family-life content.
- Bersamamu extends Kadi/Naib Kadi contact from before solemnisation through the first two years and permits referral to national agencies.

**Does not support**

- that Singapore has solved low fertility;
- that marriage preparation causes higher fertility;
- that Singapore's institutional design transfers directly to Thailand.

**Use**

Supports the claim that many conventional policy inputs already coexist in a high-capacity system and that a faith-linked premarital contact can coexist with later mentoring/referral.

---

### Norway / Nordic family policy

**Source**

OECD. *Exploring Norway's Fertility, Work, and Family Policy Trends* (2023).

**Direct support**

- Norway has comprehensive paid leave and affordable universal early-childhood education/care.
- Policy goals include safe economic/social conditions, child development, family well-being and work-care reconciliation.
- Total fertility nevertheless declined markedly from 2009 to 2022.
- Even comprehensive systems can contain access/timing gaps.

**Does not support**

- that family policy is ineffective;
- that low fertility is caused by one missing service;
- that Norway is a direct analogue for Thailand.

**Use**

Supports:
\[
Comprehensive\ family\ support \neq automatic\ fertility\ restoration
\]

---

### Korea

**Source**

Choi S, Ham S, Yang Y, Pareliussen J. *Women's employment and fertility in Korea: A literature review*. OECD, 2024.

**Direct support**

- childcare and parental-leave policies have not been sufficient to make work and motherhood compatible for all women;
- effects vary by labour-market position and access to quality childcare/support;
- employment-family incompatibility remains important.

**Use**

Supports conversion heterogeneity:
\[
Policy\ provision \neq equal\ constraint\ removal
\]

---

### Japan

**Source**

OECD. *Economic Surveys: Japan 2024*.

**Direct support**

- fertility conditions are tied to work-life balance, parental-leave take-up, childcare, non-regular employment and wider labour-market structure;
- policy response is multipronged.

**Use**

Supports embedding family policy inside a wider socioeconomic conversion environment.

## B. Access, take-up and conversion

### Penchansky & Thomas (1981)

**Source**

Penchansky R, Thomas JW. *The concept of access: definition and relationship to consumer satisfaction*. Medical Care. 1981;19(2):127–140. DOI: 10.1097/00005650-198102000-00001.

**Direct support**

Access is not a single undifferentiated property; fit between users and services has multiple dimensions.

**Use**

Prevents claiming “availability ≠ access” as an original insight.

---

### Levesque, Harris & Russell (2013)

**Source**

Levesque JF, Harris MF, Russell G. *Patient-centred access to health care: conceptualising access at the interface of health systems and populations*. International Journal for Equity in Health. 2013;12:18.

**Direct support**

Access is a process at the interface between service characteristics and people's abilities to perceive, seek, reach, pay and engage.

**Use**

Supports opening the hidden chain between formal availability and realised service use.

---

### Administrative burden

**Seminal / major sources**

- Moynihan D, Herd P, Harvey H. *Administrative Burden: Learning, Psychological, and Compliance Costs in Citizen-State Interactions*. JPART. 2015;25(1):43–69.
- Herd P, Moynihan D. *Administrative Burdens in the Social Safety Net*. Journal of Economic Perspectives. 2025;39(1):129–150.

**Direct support**

Public programmes can remain formally available while learning costs, compliance costs and psychological costs reduce actual access/take-up; burdens can reinforce inequality.

**Use**

Adds an external literature base for:
- entry burden;
- discoverability;
- paperwork/time cost;
- psychological cost;
- inequality of conversion.

**Does not support**

- a specific premarital-course effect without direct study.

---

### Capability and conversion factors

**Sources**

- Robeyns I, Byskov M. *The Capability Approach*. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, 2025.
- Yerkes MA et al. *Conceptualizing National Family Policies: A Capabilities Approach*.
- Health-promotion applications of the capability approach.

**Direct support**

Resources/public goods do not automatically become real opportunities; conversion depends on personal, social and environmental conditions. Capabilities differ from achieved functionings.

**Use**

Provides an established external intellectual lineage for:

\[
Resources \neq Capability \neq Achievement
\]

and reinforces Toledo's non-collapse discipline.

## C. Relationship preparation

### Hawkins et al. (2008)

Meta-analysis of marriage and relationship education.

**Direct support**

- average positive effects on communication and relationship-quality outcomes in experimental studies;
- moderate-dose programmes showed larger effects than low-dose programmes;
- important gaps existed for disadvantaged populations;
- stability and aggression were rarely assessed.

**Does not support**

- universal effects;
- safety outcomes;
- fertility effects.

---

### Fawcett et al. (2010)

Meta-analysis of premarital education.

**Direct support**

- communication effects were more favorable;
- relationship quality/satisfaction results were mixed and sensitive to unpublished evidence;
- longer-term prevention evidence was limited.

**Use**

Supports keeping:
\[
Course\ completion \neq guaranteed\ relationship\ outcome
\]

and cautions against assuming that more premarital education automatically produces broad benefits.

---

### Spencer & Anderson (2021)

Meta-analysis of online relationship education.

**Direct support**

Online programmes showed improvements across several relationship and individual outcomes in the included studies.

**Use**

Supports digital/self-paced delivery as plausible, but not as a replacement for all other services.

## D. Safe space, reproductive coercion and confidential access

### WHO clinical handbook

**Source**

World Health Organization. *Health care for women subjected to intimate partner violence or sexual violence: a clinical handbook*. 2014.

**Direct support**

First-line response should attend to emotional/psychological needs, physical needs, ongoing safety and ongoing support; privacy/confidentiality and safe response are central to violence care.

---

### Reproductive coercion

**Sources**

- Grace KT, Anderson JC. *Reproductive Coercion: A Systematic Review*. Trauma, Violence, & Abuse.
- Graham M et al. *Re-defining reproductive coercion using a socio-ecological lens: a scoping review*. BMC Public Health. 2023.
- American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists. *Reproductive and Sexual Coercion*.

**Direct support**

Reproductive coercion can include birth-control sabotage, pregnancy pressure/coercion and control over pregnancy outcomes. Clinical guidance recommends screening in a safe and confidential environment.

**Use**

Strengthens the need to keep reproductive/fertility discussion separate from partner-visible access when coercion is possible.

**Does not support**

- screening every person with the same instrument in every setting;
- diagnosing coercion from relationship dissatisfaction alone.

## E. Family navigation and re-entry

### Family navigation reviews

**Source example**

2025 scoping reviews of family navigation for children with autism report navigation activities including assessment, service coordination, psychosocial support, education, advocacy and logistical assistance, with preliminary evidence for improving service access/utilisation.

**Direct support**

Navigation can be an intervention function distinct from delivering the underlying specialist service.

**Does not support**

- direct transfer of autism-navigation effects to premarital populations;
- the exact re-entry architecture proposed here.

**Use**

Supports the conceptual separation:

\[
navigation \neq specialist\ treatment
\]

and supplies a field to search for referral completion and service-access outcomes.

---

### Just-in-time adaptive interventions

**Source**

Annual Review of Psychology review, 2025; plus current systematic reviews.

**Direct support**

Interventions can be designed around changing needs and timing rather than fixed delivery, but engagement remains a major challenge.

**Use**

Provides a nearby methodological literature for timing and later re-entry.

**Does not support**

- that a just-in-time digital model is appropriate for all family/safety contexts;
- that digital access equals safe access.

## F. Time scarcity and care burden

### OECD time-poverty evidence

OECD well-being work documents that the presence of children substantially increases time poverty, often more for women, and links heavy paid + unpaid workloads to stress and constrained opportunities.

### Korea fertility-intention example

A 2024 Korean study explicitly examines time poverty and women's fertility intentions across pre-/post-COVID periods.

**Use**

Provides a starting literature for testing whether time constraints should be an explicit conversion variable.

**Caution**

This does not yet directly validate the proposed urgent-versus-planned premarital distinction. That remains a priority search area.

## G. Family load, economic pressure and child development

### Family stress literature

**Sources**

- Masarik AS, Conger RD. *Stress and child development: a review of the Family Stress Model*. Current Opinion in Psychology. 2017.
- Barnett MA. *Economic Disadvantage in Complex Family Systems: Expansion of Family Stress Models*. Clinical Child and Family Psychology Review.

**Direct support**

Economic hardship can translate into economic pressure, parental distress, couple conflict/relationship problems, disrupted parenting and child outcomes through mediated pathways.

**Use**

Provides external evidence that family stress/load should not be interpreted merely as individual coping failure.

**Important distinction**

This literature should be used to support causal/mediational pathways where longitudinal evidence exists, not to literalize Toledo's conservation equation as a measured family-energy law.

## H. Islamic and Christian premarital systems

### Singapore Muslim system

Official ROMM / Our Marriage Journey materials provide a high-quality public-system example:

- marriage preparation;
- Kadi/Naib Kadi contact;
- pre-solemnisation face-to-face session;
- support through first two years;
- referral to national agencies when needed.

This is currently the strongest international faith-linked institutional comparison in the evidence set.

### Malaysia

Charoenwong (2025), *Islamic Premarital Education in Malaysia Through the Lens of Moral Education*, examines Malaysian Islamic premarital education through official documents, observation and interviews and identifies areas for development.

### Indonesia

Recent studies document government/religious premarital counselling and post-marital/conflict support through KUA/BP4 settings, but quality and design vary and much of the literature is local.

### Christian systems

Current search has identified comparative Indonesian work involving Catholic marriage preparation and Protestant premarital counselling, but the global evidence base still needs a higher-authority search.

**Status**

- Islamic comparative evidence: **partially seeded**
- Christian comparative evidence: **still weak / priority**
- direct evidence for cross-system referral interfaces: **priority**

# Round-1 consequences for the equation

## Survives provisionally

The following distinctions have strong external lineage:

\[
Resources \neq Access
\]

\[
Access \neq Capability
\]

\[
Formal\ eligibility \neq take\text{-}up
\]

\[
Navigation \neq specialist\ service
\]

\[
Relationship\ stability \neq sufficient\ safety\ readout
\]

The international policy record also supports keeping fertility as a distal, context-dependent outcome rather than a direct readout of any single family-policy input.

## Still weak and must be attacked harder

1. urgent versus non-urgent marriage preparation;
2. the exact operational definition of **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย** outside clinical violence services;
3. the value added by persistent re-entry after premarital contact;
4. cross-religious evidence on premarital systems;
5. whether trusted institutional legitimacy measurably improves uptake;
6. how to operationalise family capability without duplicating existing capability/family-policy literature;
7. how to measure repair/load without over-formalising metaphor;
8. downstream links to child/human development;
9. adverse effects of mandatory or burdensome premarital requirements.

No general-audience translation should be written until these weaker sections receive at least one strong supporting source, one current synthesis where available, and a serious search for contradictory evidence.
