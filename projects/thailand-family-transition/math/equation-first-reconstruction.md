# Equation-first reconstruction: low fertility, marriage preparation, and family-support systems

## Working status

This is a **technical working file for a conceptual paper**.

It begins with equations and system structure before ordinary-language explanation. It does **not** name a new theory, create a branded acronym, or claim novelty in advance. The structure is exposed so that readers can judge it themselves.

Workflow:

\`\`\`text
reconstruct the observed problem
→ reconstruct policy responses already used internationally
→ write the implicit policy equation
→ identify where different states have been collapsed
→ reopen those states with Toledo constraints
→ write an expanded equation system
→ attack every term with global evidence
→ retain counterevidence and boundary conditions
→ only then translate into ordinary prose
\`\`\`

Equations marked **[TOLEDO]** are constrained by the current Toledo canonical registry.  
Equations marked **[PROPOSAL]** are analytical proposals for examination, not estimated empirical laws.

# 1. Observable problem

Let:

- \(B_t\) = births in period \(t\)
- \(D_t\) = deaths in period \(t\)
- \(F_t\) = fertility readout
- \(P_t\) = population structure

A minimal demographic signal is:

\[
B_t < D_t
\tag{1}
\]

Thailand's 2026 population report records 416,574 births and 559,684 deaths in 2025 and frames the response not only around births but around investment in human quality and a family-supportive ecosystem.

The public objective therefore cannot safely be reduced to:

\[
\max F_t
\tag{2}
\]

A broader objective keeps at least four outcomes separate:

\[
\text{family formation opportunity}
+
\text{safety/autonomy}
+
\text{family well-being}
+
\text{human development}
\tag{3}
\]

# 2. Reconstruct the policy equation already used in high-capacity systems

Governments do not necessarily publish the following equation. It reconstructs the logic embedded in policy portfolios in Singapore and other OECD countries.

Define a policy-input vector:

\[
U_t =
\langle
M_t,\,
H_t,\,
L_t,\,
C_t,\,
R_t,\,
E_t,\,
S_t
\rangle
\tag{4}
\]

where:

- \(M_t\) = money / direct family benefits
- \(H_t\) = housing support
- \(L_t\) = parental leave, childcare leave, work-time protection
- \(C_t\) = childcare and early-childhood services
- \(R_t\) = reproductive-health, preconception, infertility and assisted-reproduction support
- \(E_t\) = marriage / relationship preparation and mentoring
- \(S_t\) = wider family, mental-health and social-service support

The usual policy logic can be represented as:

\[
F_{t+1}
=
\mathcal{G}
\left(
F_t,\,
U_t,\,
X_t
\right)
\tag{5}
\]

where \(X_t\) includes labour markets, norms, age structure, housing conditions and macroeconomic context.

A practical response to low fertility is often:

\[
F_t \downarrow
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
U_t \uparrow
\tag{6}
\]

## 2.1 Singapore: many coordinates already activated

Singapore currently combines:

- child-related financial support;
- childcare leave and employer reimbursement;
- 10 weeks of shared parental leave for eligible births from 1 April 2026;
- preschool and housing support;
- public co-funding for assisted conception procedures;
- marriage preparation covering communication, conflict resolution, finances, roles, family planning/fertility awareness and in-law relationships;
- online and bite-sized marriage-learning options;
- Muslim marriage preparation through Cinta Abadi;
- Bersamamu contact with a Kadi/Naib Kadi before solemnisation and through the first two years of marriage, including referral to national agencies where needed.

So the problem is not simply that advanced systems have failed to provide money, leave, childcare, fertility treatment or marriage preparation.

## 2.2 Norway: comprehensive support without automatic fertility restoration

Norway combines generous parental leave, affordable universal early-childhood education and care, cash support and work-family reconciliation. OECD still documents a substantial fertility decline.

Therefore:

\[
\text{Comprehensive family support}
\neq
\text{automatic restoration of replacement fertility}
\tag{7}
\]

This does not imply that family support is ineffective, because child development, family well-being and work-care reconciliation are also valid outcomes.

## 2.3 Korea: policy quantity does not guarantee conversion

OECD reviews of Korea conclude that childcare and parental-leave policies have not been sufficient to make employment and motherhood compatible for all groups, and effects vary by labour-market position and access to support.

Thus:

\[
U_t \uparrow
\not\Rightarrow
\text{constraint removal for every group}
\tag{8}
\]

## 2.4 Japan: family policy sits inside a larger labour/housing system

OECD's Japan review links fertility to work-life balance, labour-market dualism, non-regular work, parental-leave take-up, childcare and wider economic conditions.

Therefore:

\[
\text{family policy}
\subset
\text{larger social-economic conversion environment}
\tag{9}
\]

## 2.5 OECD-wide evidence

OECD cross-country analysis finds associations between fertility and employment, parental leave, early-childhood education and care, financial supports and housing, but also stresses that country context matters and family policy alone does not explain fertility decline.

The international record therefore supports the caution:

\[
U_t \uparrow
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
F_{t+1} \uparrow
\tag{10}
\]

The hidden transformation between policy supply and lived outcome must be opened.

# 6. Effective access: formal provision is not enough

Let \(A^{formal}_{i,t}\) be nominal service availability.

Define:

\[
A^{eff}_{i,t}
=
\mathcal{A}
\left(
A^{formal}_{i,t},
N_{i,t},
T_{i,t},
S_{i,t},
R_{i,t},
L_{i,t}
\right)
\tag{31}
\]

where:

- \(N\) = navigation/discoverability
- \(T\) = temporal fit
- \(S\) = safe/private accessibility
- \(R\) = referral continuity
- \(L\) = institutional/cultural legitimacy

Do not assume an arbitrary multiplicative empirical form before evidence. Require only:

\[
\frac{\partial A^{eff}}{\partial z_k}\ge0
\tag{32}
\]

for a genuinely support-enhancing dimension \(z_k\), and where a dimension is necessary:

\[
z_k\to0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
A^{eff}\to0
\tag{33}
\]

Example: a violence-response service may formally exist while effective safe access approaches zero for a person whose partner controls the only visible channel.

# 14A. Capacity-aware edge/node diagnosis

Navigation can improve access by making previously hidden demand visible.

For service node \(j\):

\[
\lambda'_j
=
\lambda_j
+
\Delta\lambda^{edge}_j
\tag{65a}
\]

where:

- \(\lambda_j\) = baseline demand reaching the service;
- \(\Delta\lambda^{edge}_j\) = additional demand reaching it after better connection;
- \(\mu_j\) = usable service capacity.

Define:

\[
Slack_j
=
\mu_j-\lambda'_j
\tag{65b}
\]

If:

\[
Slack_j>0
\]

and the dominant barrier is search/referral/coordination, improving the edge is plausible.

If:

\[
Slack_j\le0
\]

edge-only improvement can increase waiting and expose a node/capacity bottleneck.

Therefore classify:

\[
Bottleneck_j
\in
\{
MissingEdge,\,
MissingNode,\,
MissingCapacity,\,
Mixed
\}
\tag{65c}
\]

and choose:

\[
a^{*}
=
\arg\max_{a\in\{Edge,Node,Hybrid\}}
\frac{\Delta Y(a)}{\Delta C(a)}
\tag{65d}
\]

subject to:

\[
Safety\ge s^{*},\quad

# 18. What would weaken this reconstruction?

Revise the equations if evidence shows that:

1. formal provision predicts take-up and realized outcomes so strongly that intermediate conversion states add little explanatory value;
2. urgent and non-urgent preparation show no meaningful difference in burden, reach or usefulness;
3. private confidential access does not alter safe disclosure/help-seeking where monitoring or coercion exists;
4. continuity/re-entry adds no observable value beyond one-time preparation;
5. institutional trust has no relationship with uptake or follow-through;
6. service fragmentation does not predict navigation burden or incomplete referral;
7. a substantially simpler equation explains the same observations with fewer states.

The mathematics should make criticism more precise, not protect the argument from criticism.
