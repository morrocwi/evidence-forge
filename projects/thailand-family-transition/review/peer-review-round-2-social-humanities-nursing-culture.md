# 2. Reviewer 1 — Social Sciences

## Decision

**Minor-to-moderate revision**

## What now works

The revised manuscript is much clearer about the unit of analysis. The move:

\[
SameProvision\rightarrow UnequalConversion
\]

adds a genuinely sociological dimension and avoids treating service access as an individual behavioural problem.

The explicit distinction between:

- micro;
- meso;
- macro

also prevents the earlier slippage from premarital education to national fertility.

## Remaining major issue — “social position” needs an empirical status

The manuscript now includes:

\[
SocialPosition_i
\]

inside the family-capability function.

This is conceptually justified, but the paper should not imply that “social position” is one measurable variable.

### Revision required

State that social position is a **relational/structural descriptor** whose operationalization depends on the empirical question.

Possible indicators may include:

- class/income;
- employment security;
- education;
- migration/legal status;
- language;
- disability;
- gendered power;
- geography/digital access.

Do not build a single social-position score unless later evidence supports it.

## Remaining issue — marriage remains institutionally privileged by sampling

Even with the new caveat, the paper's architecture will mostly reach people who encounter marriage-related institutions.

That creates a selection boundary:

\[
PremaritalArchitecture
\not\Rightarrow
UniversalFamilyArchitecture
\]

### Revision required

State explicitly that this approach will not reach:

- people forming families without marriage;
- people who avoid formal institutions;
- some highly marginalized groups;
- people whose marriage is already completed before they enter Thai services.

This is a scope limitation, not a reason to abandon the paper.

# 3. Reviewer 2 — Humanities

## Decision

**Minor revision**

## What now works

The manuscript now distinguishes:

\[
MeaningAsConversionFactor
\]

from:

\[
MeaningAsValuedEnd
\]

which resolves the earlier tendency to treat culture mainly as an engagement tool.

The explicit right to dissent from community norms is an important improvement.

## Remaining issue — “capability” and “valued life” need one cleaner paragraph

The paper currently has capability language in several places. It risks making the reader reconstruct the relationship between:

- opportunity;
- choice;
- meaning;
- valued ends;
- actual outcomes.

### Revision required

Add one compact clarification:

> Capability concerns the genuine set of opportunities available to a person; meaning concerns which of those possibilities the person values; realized action is what is actually chosen or becomes possible under context and constraint. These should not be collapsed.

Then retain:

\[
Capability\neq Preference\neq RealizedAction
\]

This is enough; do not add more philosophy.

## Remaining issue — avoid translating all human meaning into service uptake

Several later sections return quickly from meaning to navigation/referral.

The final discussion should preserve the possibility that good premarital support can result in **no service uptake** because the person makes an informed decision that no additional service is desired.

Therefore:

\[
NoReferral
\neq
Failure
\]

when informed choice and safety are preserved.

# 4. Reviewer 3 — Nursing / Family Health

## Decision

**Moderate revision**

## What now works

The dedicated nursing section substantially improves journal fit.

The manuscript appropriately positions nursing in:

- preconception health;
- health literacy;
- reproductive-risk recognition within scope;
- confidential enquiry;
- mental-health recognition;
- referral;
- continuity.

The boundary:

\[
Nursing/CommunityInterface
\neq
SpecialistSubstitution
\]

is especially important.

## Remaining major issue — nursing needs to appear earlier

At present nursing relevance enters mainly in the later implications section.

For a nursing journal, reviewers may ask why the manuscript belongs in nursing before they reach Section 20.

### Revision required

Add 2–3 sentences in the introduction stating that premarital contact is relevant to nursing because nurses already work across reproductive health, primary care, family health, mental health recognition, safeguarding, pregnancy/postpartum, and child/family continuity.

Do not turn the whole article into a nursing-only argument; simply establish disciplinary relevance early.

## Remaining issue — distinguish routine enquiry from universal screening claims

The safeguarding section should avoid language that might imply a universal screening recommendation without regard to guideline/context.

### Revision required

Use wording such as:

> confidential enquiry, risk recognition, and referral according to applicable clinical guidance and service capacity

rather than suggesting one mandatory screening tool for every premarital user.

## Remaining issue — clinical outcome hierarchy is good; move it into a table

The proximal/intermediate/distal outcome hierarchy is one of the most useful parts for nursing readers.

### Revision required

Convert it into one concise table in the final formatted manuscript.

# 5. Reviewer 4 — Cultural Studies

## Decision

**Minor revision**

## What now works

The manuscript now explicitly rejects:

\[
Thailand=SingleCulturalContext
\]

and adds:

\[
CulturalLegitimacy\neq IndividualTrust
\]

\[
CommunityAuthority\neq PersonalConsent
\]

This substantially reduces essentialism.

## Remaining issue — cultural institutions should not be framed mainly as low-cost assets

The paper still sometimes moves from “culturally embedded node” toward “low-burden/low-cost entry point”.

That can unintentionally instrumentalize religious or community institutions as delivery infrastructure for state policy.

### Revision required

Add one sentence that culturally embedded institutions have their own purposes, moral worlds, and accountabilities and should not be reduced to delivery channels for demographic or health policy.

A common interface should therefore be negotiated, not imposed.

## Remaining issue — intercultural/transnational couples need a single concrete analytical sentence

The current mention is good but brief.

### Revision required

State that these couples expose the architecture particularly clearly because:

\[
LegalNavigation
+
Language
+
KinshipExpectations
+
CulturalMeaning
+
MigrationStatus
\]

may all affect effective access simultaneously.

No further migration-theory expansion is needed.

# 6. Cross-disciplinary editorial issue — manuscript architecture

The manuscript now has a strong conceptual core but too many top-level sections for a journal article.

Current structure is analytically rich but reads partly like a monograph or technical dossier.

## Required compression

For final submission, compress the visible article into approximately the following Mahidol-style academic-article spine:

2. **ขอบเขตและวิธีการสังเคราะห์องค์ความรู้**

9. **วาระวิจัยและข้อจำกัด**

# 9. Length and redundancy

Current repository-level manuscript size:

- approximately 61,000 characters;
- more than 1,100 markdown lines;
- 54 headings/subheadings;
- 42 numbered references currently used.

A character count is not equivalent to the journal's formal word count, especially for Thai text.

Therefore the manuscript **cannot yet be certified as within the journal word limit** using repository text alone.

## Main redundancy targets

The following ideas are repeated several times:

- fertility is distal;
- premarital is not a universal course;
- Singapore is not to be copied;
- private access is needed for some risk states;
- culture is not religion;
- navigation does not replace capacity;
- civil society does not replace structural policy.

Each should be fully explained once and referred back to later rather than re-argued.

Likely reduction target:

\[
15\%-25\%
\]

of body text without loss of substantive content.

# 10. Recommended final central thesis

> Premarital should be studied not as a universal curriculum or the presumed beginning of family life, but as a marriage-related institutional contact zone in which socially unequal persons and couples may—or may not—convert existing resources into usable opportunities. Whether this contact contributes to later family well-being depends on effective access, temporal feasibility, safety and autonomy, cultural meaning, and the capacity and governance of receiving services.

Thai working formulation:

> ช่วงก่อนสมรสไม่ควรถูกมองเป็นหลักสูตรมาตรฐานหรือเป็นจุดเริ่มต้นของครอบครัวสำหรับทุกคน แต่เป็นพื้นที่สัมผัสกับสถาบันที่เกี่ยวข้องกับการสมรส ซึ่งบุคคลและคู่ที่มีฐานทางสังคมไม่เท่ากันอาจสามารถ—หรือไม่สามารถ—เปลี่ยนทรัพยากรที่มีอยู่ให้เป็นโอกาสที่ใช้ได้จริง ทั้งนี้ขึ้นกับการเข้าถึง เวลา ความปลอดภัยและอำนาจตัดสินใจ ความหมายทางวัฒนธรรม ตลอดจนความจุและธรรมาภิบาลของบริการปลายทาง
