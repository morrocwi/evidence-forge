# Synthesis — Safety and governance guardrails

## Status

**Type:** safety-oriented synthesis / design requirements  
**Primary-source verification still required for the embedded IPV literature in S004.**

## Core distinction

```text
relationship stability != safety
staying married != successful intervention
```

A family-support or premarital system should not define success only as relationship persistence or divorce reduction.

## Why this matters

A shared couple-facing intervention may be inappropriate when one partner experiences:

- fear
- coercive control
- violence
- sexual coercion
- reproductive coercion
- severe distress

In such cases, a relationship-improvement objective may conflict with safety.

## Minimum design separation

```text
Couple channel
├── education
├── planning
├── shared goals
├── communication tools
└── parenting / finance modules

Private individual channel
├── fear / coercion
├── violence
├── sexual or reproductive coercion
├── severe distress
├── confidential referral
└── safe-exit information
```

Information entered in a private pathway should not automatically be visible to the partner.

## Governance principle

When immediate danger or coercive control is present:

```text
relationship improvement
        ↓ stop being the primary objective
safety + professional response
        ↓
appropriate referral / protection
```

## Candidate outcomes

Potential safety-compatible outcomes include:

- safe disclosure
- professional help-seeking
- successful referral
- access to protection
- temporary separation where appropriate
- safe exit where appropriate

## Measurement warning

Avoid using any of the following alone as success indicators:

- course completion
- satisfaction
- staying married
- lower divorce counts

## Evidence boundary

This file records a design and governance principle derived from the current working synthesis. It does not establish the prevalence of violence among premarital participants or the effectiveness of a specific Thai safety pathway.
