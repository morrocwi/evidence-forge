# Synthesis — Fragmentation hypothesis

## Status

**Type:** cross-source synthesis / research hypothesis  
**Not an established causal finding.**

## Evidence inputs

- **S001**: Thailand's low-fertility response is framed in terms of family-supportive systems and human development, not only birth counts.
- **S002**: Mahidol Model provides a Thai precedent for integrating family, community, school, and health-service nodes around one problem.
- **S004**: the working service-landscape review identifies relevant functions spread across civil registration, public health, family services, faith-based systems, mental health, violence response, and parenting/child-development services.
- **S003**: infertility care illustrates that reproductive health also has psychosocial and social-support dimensions.

## Working synthesis

The central system-level hypothesis is:

```text
Thailand may not primarily lack relevant services.
It may lack continuity and navigation across services that activate at different life stages.
```

This should be tested rather than asserted as settled fact.

## Proposed fragmentation dimensions

```text
FRAGMENTATION
├── timing
│   ├── before marriage
│   ├── preconception
│   ├── pregnancy
│   ├── parenting
│   └── crisis / violence
├── institutional ownership
├── eligibility
├── content
├── access channel
├── data / continuity
└── referral pathway
```

## Candidate service problem

A couple or individual may encounter multiple useful systems but still need to discover them independently and re-enter different institutions as needs change.

## Researchable questions

1. Which premarital/family-related services actually exist by province and institution?
2. Who reaches them and who does not?
3. At what life stage does each service become visible?
4. Are referral pathways formal, informal, or absent?
5. Can users return to support after the premarital period without repeating intake?
6. Does a low-burden navigation layer improve referral completion?
7. Does improved navigation change family, health, or safety outcomes?

## Falsification conditions

The fragmentation hypothesis would be weakened if a national mapping showed that:

- services are already linked through standardized pathways,
- users can reliably navigate them,
- referral completion is high,
- and continuity is maintained across life stages.

## Manuscript use

Use this synthesis in:

Do **not** write "Thailand has no integrated system" unless national evidence directly establishes that claim.
