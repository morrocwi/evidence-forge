# Existing-to-expanded model — from premarital course to lifelong family support

## Core position

The proposed framework should **not replace** existing Islamic, Christian, public-health, or family-service premarital systems.

The design question is:

> How can systems that already prepare people for marriage be expanded from a one-time course or ceremony-linked requirement into a low-burden, safe, re-enterable support pathway across family life?

## Existing provision already identified in Thailand

The current Thailand service review identifies:

### Islamic settings

Public evidence shows premarital preparation in some provincial Islamic systems, including:

- premarital training before nikah
- Islamic marriage rules
- roles of spouses
- talaq / iddah / reconciliation / fasakh
- child-rearing and causes of family breakdown in some local curricula

The review also identifies prior Thai research calling for improvement in areas such as women's participation among instructors and gender-rights content.

### Christian settings

The review identifies a Thai quasi-experimental programme, “Two Become One,” aimed at developing knowledge and skills of premarital counsellors.

### Public-health settings

Thailand already has premarital / preconception content focused on:

- reproductive health
- infectious-disease screening
- thalassaemia
- folate
- pregnancy readiness
- fertility / infertility services

## The proposed change is therefore an **upgrade and extension**, not replacement.

| Dimension | Existing / conventional model | Expanded model proposed for testing |
|---|---|---|
| Main timing | Before marriage | Before marriage + re-entry across later life events |
| Main endpoint | Complete course / certificate / ceremony readiness | Navigation literacy + relationship readiness + health readiness + safe access to help |
| Pace | Usually one defined course | Two pathways: urgent and non-urgent |
| Content | Institution-specific curriculum | Preserve core faith/health content + modular cross-cutting content |
| Health | Often separate or referral-dependent | Explicit preconception / fertility / mental-health bridge |
| Safety | May be implicit or variable | Explicit **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe space** + private confidential pathway |
| Couple format | Mostly couple/classroom | Couple channel **and** individual private channel |
| Help after marriage | Often outside course scope | Persistent re-entry: conflict, fertility, pregnancy, parenting, crisis |
| Referral | Variable | Visible referral map and completion tracking |
| Success metric | Attendance / completion / satisfaction | Reach, understanding, help-seeking, referral completion, safety, relationship and health outcomes |
| Governance | Course owner | Networked governance across faith/community/health/social services |
| Digital role | Optional information | Access key, micro-learning, re-entry, navigation, referral |
| Diversity | Curriculum may assume one family pathway | Life-course modules activated only when relevant |

## Urgent versus non-urgent marriage preparation

A key extension is to stop assuming that all couples have the same amount of preparation time.

### Path A — Urgent / rapid marriage preparation

Use when the marriage timeline is short.

**Goal:** minimum safe and informed readiness without creating a barrier to marriage.

Suggested core exposure:

```text
10–30 minute core orientation
    ↓
consent / expectations / key responsibilities
    ↓
relationship warning signs
    ↓
basic reproductive & preconception navigation
    ↓
safe space / confidential help route
    ↓
persistent access key for later modules
```

The rapid pathway should **not** attempt to compress a long course into one sitting.

The minimum product is:

- know what must be discussed now
- know what can wait
- know where to return
- know how to seek private help safely

### Path B — Non-urgent / planned marriage preparation

Use when couples have sufficient preparation time.

Possible sequence:

```text
core orientation
  -> communication/conflict
  -> roles and care work
  -> finance/debt
  -> sexual and reproductive health
  -> fertility/preconception
  -> family of origin / in-laws
  -> parenting expectations
  -> intercultural/interfaith issues where relevant
  -> safety/coercion recognition
  -> personal consultation where needed
  -> referral
```

This pathway can provide deeper learning, practice, reflection, and counselling without making the urgent pathway inaccessible.

## Faith-based expansion without erasing religious identity

The architecture should use a **core + extension** principle.

### Islamic programme

Keep:

- Islamic foundations of marriage
- nikah requirements
- rights and responsibilities
- family ethics
- talaq / reconciliation / fasakh where already taught
- parenting and family responsibility

Add or strengthen:

- communication practice
- financial expectations and debt
- reproductive and preconception health referral
- mental-health literacy
- coercion and violence recognition
- women's access to confidential consultation
- **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe space**
- post-nikah re-entry and referral

### Christian programme

Keep:

- theological meaning of marriage
- covenant / commitment framing where relevant to denomination
- pastoral preparation
- communication and family-life content already present

Add or strengthen:

- structured health and preconception referral
- mental-health and relationship-risk recognition
- confidential individual access
- **พื้นที่ปลอดภัย / safe space**
- safeguarding and violence pathways
- persistent post-marriage re-entry
- referral beyond pastoral care when specialist support is needed

## Important governance boundary

Faith leaders should not be expected to become:

- physicians
- fertility specialists
- psychotherapists
- domestic-violence responders
- lawyers

The expanded system should make them better **recognition and referral nodes**, while preserving the spiritual and cultural role of faith-based preparation.

## Research proposition

The question is not:

> “Should religious premarital courses be replaced?”

It is:

> “Can existing trusted premarital institutions become durable entry and re-entry nodes into a broader family-support ecosystem without losing their identity or creating excessive burden?”

That proposition should be tested through implementation research.
