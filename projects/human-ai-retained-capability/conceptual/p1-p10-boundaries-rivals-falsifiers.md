# P1–P10 boundary conditions, rival explanations and falsifiers

These are theory-strengthening devices for an **academic / conceptual article**. They are not claims that the proposed experiments have been run.

## P1 — Problem ownership should change how learners remain with inquiry

**Boundary conditions**
- degree of scaffolding / guidance
- prior knowledge and age
- whether the problem is genuinely open or only cosmetically open
- individual versus collaborative inquiry
- stakes / authenticity / personal relevance
- time available for iterative framing

**Rival explanations**
- effects attributed to ownership may actually be caused by intrinsic interest, novelty, prior knowledge, teacher attention or greater time-on-task.
- open tasks may increase engagement while reducing domain learning if guidance is inadequate.

**Falsifier**
If ownership is experimentally varied while scaffolding, prior knowledge, topic interest and time are controlled, and sustained inquiry / reasoned reframing do not differ, P1 is weakened. If pre-shaped tasks outperform owned problems on the paper's own trajectory measures, P1 must be narrowed.

## P2 — Key terms should alter the route set proposed by AI

**Boundary conditions**
- model and version
- decoding / sampling settings
- semantic distance between substituted terms
- task type and domain
- whether originating facts are held constant
- evaluation method used to judge route divergence

**Rival explanations**
- apparent route changes may be random sampling variance.
- apparent prompt sensitivity may be created by brittle evaluation rules.
- term substitution may alter factual meaning rather than merely open another lens.

**Falsifier**
Across repeated runs with facts and semantics controlled, pivotal-term substitutions produce no systematic increase in domain/tool/workflow route divergence beyond baseline paraphrase variance.

---

## P3 — AI may reduce orientation cost faster than it produces expertise

**Boundary conditions**
- domain tacitness and physical/practical requirements
- task complexity and risk
- novice versus experienced user
- access to real practice, coaching and feedback
- whether "orientation" means vocabulary recognition, source finding, workflow mapping or interactional fluency

**Rival explanations**
- observed gains may be ordinary information-search efficiency, not orientation toward expertise.
- in highly codified domains, AI tutoring may produce genuine skill rapidly enough that the proposed gap is small.
- task scores may make novices appear expert while masking unsupported judgment.

**Falsifier**
After matched AI exposure, novices show stable practitioner-level performance, transfer, error detection and appropriate boundary judgment under domain constraints, with no meaningful lag between orientation and expertise acquisition.

---

## P4 — Good cross-domain routing should reveal tools without deleting important facts

**Boundary conditions**
- whether the task has objective safety/legal/power constraints
- relevance of the "preserved" facts to the new domain
- cognitive load from carrying many constraints
- reversible exploratory tasks versus consequential action
- completeness and accuracy of the original problem representation

**Rival explanations**
- some facts can be legitimately abstracted away when moving domains.
- preserving every original fact may block productive abstraction.
- quality may depend more on expert evaluation after routing than on initial route preservation.

**Falsifier**
Routes that omit declared critical constraints perform as well as or better than constraint-preserving routes on wrong-domain routing, decision quality and real-case return, without increased harms or omissions.

---

## P5 — Knowledge-like candidates are not learning; good artifacts are not potential

**Boundary conditions**
- assistance mode: answer generation, critique, explanation, tutoring, feedback
- baseline expertise
- cognitive engagement / active reconstruction
- delay before assessment
- original-task versus transfer assessment
- task complexity and domain
- whether AI remains available during the outcome measure

**Rival explanations**
- high artifact quality may predict later learning strongly in some scaffolded conditions.
- apparent performance–learning gaps may be caused by differences in time-on-task or motivation rather than AI.
- AI can sometimes free time for reading, comparison and explanation, producing delayed gains.

**Falsifier**
Across appropriately controlled contexts, assisted artifact quality consistently predicts delayed unaided retention and transfer so strongly that measuring learner status separately adds negligible explanatory or predictive value.

**Key strengthening**
P5 should be framed as a **non-equivalence / measurement-separation proposition**, not as "AI harms learning."

---

## P6 — Dissonance should interrupt recursive confirmation only when treated as work requiring checking

**Boundary conditions**
- whether the learner notices the discrepancy
- prior knowledge and epistemic beliefs
- perceived stakes and accountability
- access to verification tools / sources
- quality of metacognitive or epistemic scaffolding
- strength and ambiguity of contradictory evidence

**Rival explanations**
- sufficiently salient contradiction may trigger conceptual change without explicit verification.
- teacher authority, social pressure or task grading may drive revision rather than reflective dissonance.
- checking may occur but fail because the verification route is weak.

**Falsifier**
Merely noticing contradiction predicts belief revision and durable conceptual change as well as active verification does, after prior knowledge, stakes and feedback are controlled.

---

## P7 — Independence of the verification route should matter more than number of answers

**Boundary conditions**
- genuine independence of data / method / evaluator
- competence and relevance of the independent route
- correlated model training data and shared sources
- task verifiability
- cost and latency of verification
- whether answer diversity reflects independent evidence or only linguistic variation

**Rival explanations**
- a sufficiently diverse ensemble may approximate independent checking if errors are weakly correlated.
- an "independent" source may share the same upstream evidence or error.
- verification quality may depend more on evaluator competence than independence.

**Falsifier**
After route competence and cost are controlled, additional genuinely independent evidence/method/evaluator routes do not improve correction or justified conclusions relative to the same number of correlated answers; or answer count explains outcomes while independence adds no incremental value.

---

## P8 — Developed potential should be visible after assistance, not only during it

**Boundary conditions**
- automation versus augmentation mode
- opportunity for retrieval, explanation and practice
- delay interval
- transfer distance
- baseline expertise
- task type
- feedback quality
- continued partial access to AI
- learner motivation and self-regulation

**Rival explanations**
- post-assistance gains may be explained entirely by ordinary retrieval practice, feedback quality, deliberate practice or time-on-task.
- fluent dialogue can itself be productive when it elicits explanation and active reasoning.
- negative post-assistance effects may reflect poor instructional design rather than AI assistance per se.

**Falsifier**
When time-on-task, feedback quality and practice are matched, cycles containing the proposed verification/practice/action/world-feedback elements do not improve delayed retention, unaided error detection or transfer over dialogue-only conditions.

**Key strengthening**
P8 should predict **heterogeneity by assistance mode**, not a universal direction of AI effect.

---

## P9 — Institutions lowering verification, practice and safe-referral cost should increase responsible potential development

**Boundary conditions**
- institutional quality and trustworthiness
- equitable access
- domain risk and regulation
- availability of qualified expertise
- transaction / waiting costs
- whether referral pathways are actually usable
- community and peer alternatives

**Rival explanations**
- institutions can increase gatekeeping, delay and inequality.
- decentralized communities or peer networks may lower verification costs more effectively.
- lower verification cost may increase action volume without improving quality.

**Falsifier**
Reducing access cost to verification, practice and referral does not increase warranted action, retained learning, safe referral or reachable-option conversion—or increases harmful/incorrect action enough to offset the benefit.

---

## P10 — A history of correction and responsible knowledge use may lead to greater entrusted participation

**Boundary conditions**
- repeated observation over time
- task-specific competence
- consequence / risk level
- supervisor or institution's authority to entrust
- adequate exposure and visibility
- relationship continuity
- structural bias, status and network position
- cultural expectations around autonomy and trust

**Rival explanations**
- entrustment may be driven mainly by credentials, reputation, social similarity, hierarchy or opportunity rather than correction history.
- competence alone may explain entrusted responsibility.
- cautious limit-disclosure may sometimes be interpreted as lower competence rather than trustworthiness.

**Falsifier**
Longitudinal evidence of correction, limit disclosure, help-seeking, reliability and responsible action adds no predictive value for increased entrusted responsibility once competence, exposure, credentials, relationship and status are controlled.

## Theory-level kill criterion

If adjacent established theories explain these relations with equal or better precision and the P1–P10 architecture yields no new boundary condition, measurement distinction, observable implication or cross-stage prediction, the framework should be reduced rather than protected by new terminology.
