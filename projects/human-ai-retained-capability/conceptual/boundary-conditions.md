# Boundary conditions

The theory must state when it should not be expected to hold.

## Candidate boundaries

### Task type
Effects may differ for recall, writing, coding, diagnosis, planning, creative generation, and physical skills.

### Baseline expertise
Novices and experts may use the same AI output differently.

### Assistance modality
Autocomplete, critique, tutoring, full answer generation, retrieval, planning, and autonomous agents create different cognitive demands.

### Reliability
A highly reliable model may encourage delegation; an unreliable model may induce verification. Either can alter retention through different mechanisms.

### Time
Immediate post-withdrawal performance is not equivalent to delayed retention.

### Stakes and motivation
Users may process assistance differently in low-stakes versus high-stakes settings.

### Repeated use
Short exposure and chronic dependence should not be assumed equivalent.

### Measurement condition
Open-book, memory-only, tool-limited, and transfer tasks measure different capabilities.

## Boundary rule

A proposition without a plausible failure condition is too broad.
