# Falsifiers

A theory that cannot lose is not useful.

## F1 — No assisted–retained distinction

If across appropriately designed studies assisted performance reliably predicts post-withdrawal capability with negligible divergence under relevant conditions, the central distinction has less explanatory value.

## F2 — Mechanism invariance

If active reconstruction, delegation, feedback quality, and verification behavior do not alter retained capability when confounds are controlled, several proposed mechanisms are weakened.

## F3 — No calibration gap

If perceived capability consistently tracks independently measured retained capability after AI use, the calibration-gap proposition is weakened.

## F4 — No transfer distinction

If original-task retention and cross-context transfer are empirically indistinguishable across relevant designs, the proposed separation of retention and conversion requires revision.

## F5 — Existing theory subsumes the model

If established learning / transfer / cognitive offloading theory fully specifies the proposed constructs and relations without loss, the project should be reframed as an application rather than a new theory.

## F6 — Unit-of-analysis failure

If individual post-withdrawal capability is irrelevant to the target phenomenon because performance is inherently system-level, the scope of the theory must be narrowed.

## Use

Each proposition should eventually reference at least one falsifier or disconfirming observation.
