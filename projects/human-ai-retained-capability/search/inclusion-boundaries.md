# Inclusion boundaries

## Include when relevant

- empirical studies of AI-assisted vs unassisted performance
- delayed or transfer tests after assistance
- cognitive offloading and automation-dependence research
- learning retention and transfer studies
- theory papers defining human agency, autonomy, extended cognition, or distributed cognition
- studies that separate immediate task success from later independent capability
- benchmark or protocol papers that allow a withdrawal / post-assistance comparison

## Exclude or downgrade

- claims based only on vendor marketing
- immediate performance studies with no relevance to retained capability, unless used only for the assisted-performance side of the distinction
- opinion pieces used as empirical evidence
- studies whose task, population, or AI modality is too different for the claim being made
- secondary citations when the primary source is available

## Scope discipline

A paper can be highly relevant to one construct and irrelevant to another. Inclusion is claim-specific, not prestige-specific.
